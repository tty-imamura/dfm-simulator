Determinacy-Field Mechanics (DFM), introduced in a companion paper, is
a two-dimensional relational toy model in which inertia, gravity, clock
rate and light propagation are all read from a single scalar field.
We ask how far such a model can be pushed \emph{toward real
numbers}---real constants, masses and orbital elements---before it
breaks, and what has to be said honestly at each step.

the correspondence lock $\kappa=G/c_0^{2}$ holds exactly.

reproduces Mercury's perihelion advance \emph{in the dynamics}, not as

expression and converts to $43.0''$/century against the observed

(P2)~The stability of the strong-dragging sector is \emph{unit-system

(P3)~With dragging on, a \emph{pure two-body} Earth--Moon system
reproduces the sidereal month and the $8.85$-year lunar apsidal cycle
simultaneously on two calibration knobs---though the real driver of
that cycle is solar perturbation, making this a calibrated match
through a different mechanism.
(P4)~One background value $D_0=0.006$ and one \emph{derivation rule}

\emph{far-field} amplitude at the Lense--Thirring scale, not a
pointwise match to it.

whereas the $q=3$ control moves two of the four outside the
pre-registered $\pm1\%$; those periods are transcribed rather than
predicted, so this is a statement about \emph{survival of an observed
configuration under a computed exponent}.
The failures that produced the conventions are reported as results, and
a calibrated match is not claimed to license more than it does:
agreement of a number is not identification of a mechanism.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The first companion paper~\cite{DFM1} introduced Determinacy-Field
Mechanics (DFM): a two-dimensional educational toy model in which
space is purely relational, every mechanical property of a point being
set by the surrounding masses through a scalar \emph{determinacy
field} $W$, from which inertia, gravity, clock rate, and light
propagation are all read.
The second~\cite{DFM2} replaced ``everything far away'' by a single
summarized \emph{box} and studied expansion, redshift semantics, and
probe-dependent expansion estimates inside it.
Both papers worked in the model's own internal units, and both were
explicit that DFM is a computational thought experiment rather than a
proposal about nature.

This paper asks a different question, and one that the earlier
internal-unit framing deliberately postponed: \emph{how far can a
relational toy universe be pushed toward real numbers before it
breaks?}
Concretely: can the real Sun and the real Mercury---real masses, real
radii, real spin rates, real perihelion distance and perihelion
speed---be placed in the toy universe unchanged, with the simulation's
$G$ and $c_0$ restoring to the SI gravitational constant and the SI
speed of light, and can the model then be asked for a number that
astronomers measure?

\subsection{Reality calibration versus mechanism identification}
\label{sec:definitions}

We use two terms throughout, and the distinction between them is the
thesis of this paper.

\emph{Reality calibration} is the operation of choosing conventions and
parameter values so that a stated observable of the model agrees with
a stated observable of the sky, together with an audit of exactly
which quantities were fixed by the choice and which were then
predicted.
A reality calibration is successful when the agreement is quantitative,
the number of adjusted quantities is smaller than the number of
matched observables, and every adjusted quantity is named.

\emph{Mechanism identification} is the far stronger claim that the
physical process producing the number in the model is the process
producing the number in nature.
Nothing in this paper is a mechanism identification.
The clearest case is the lunar apsidal cycle of
Sec.~\ref{sec:p3}: DFM reproduces the observed $8.85$-year period in a
system containing only two bodies, whereas the real cycle is driven
principally by solar perturbation---a third body that the model does
not contain.
The agreement is real, reproducible, and machine-checked; it is also,
demonstrably, produced by a different mechanism.
We therefore report it as a calibrated match and say so in the same
breath in which we report the number.

This distinction is not a disclaimer appended to the results.
It is what makes the results usable: a toy model that says which of
its agreements are calibrations and which are consequences is a
teaching instrument, whereas one that does not is a source of
confusion.

\subsection{Chronology as method}
\label{sec:chronology}

The four pillars of this paper (P1--P4) are presented in the order in
which they were measured, because each arose from the failure of the
convention that preceded it.

P1 (Sec.~\ref{sec:p1}) had been declared out of scope twice.
Under the earlier convention---an exaggerated analogue speed of light
$c_0=30$---Mercury's dimensionless depth $GM/(c^{2}a)=2.55\times
10^{-8}$ required a mass outside the model's admissible range, and
even after that was fixed by a real-$c$ convention, the single-precision
state representation rounded the 1PN signal away.
Only the third attempt, with a double-precision state carry, succeeded.

P2 (Sec.~\ref{sec:p2}) is a negative result promoted to a structural
statement: when free binaries with full dragging collapsed, the
quantity that organized the measurements turned out to be a loop gain
assembled from the \emph{dimensional} weight $w=m/d$, so that the
stability of a configuration depends on the declared unit system.
Every subsequent calibration inherits that fact.

P3 (Sec.~\ref{sec:p3}) came from asking whether the collapse-prone
sector could nevertheless be calibrated rather than merely avoided.
It could, and the calibrated observable turned out to be one that a
two-body model has no right to possess.

P4 (Sec.~\ref{sec:p4}) came from the failure of P3's calibration to
transfer: the value of $D_0$ that calibrated the Earth--Moon system was
seven orders of magnitude away from the value that calibrated Mercury.
That tension was first resolved by a joint correction in which a
\emph{second} parameter absorbed the mismatch, and then dissolved
altogether when that second parameter was shown to be computable from
known quantities, leaving no adjustable exponent at all.

Section~\ref{sec:conv} fixes the scale-conversion conventions used
everywhere below.
Sections~\ref{sec:p1}--\ref{sec:p4} present P1--P4.
Section~\ref{sec:ring} states the scope of the ring-system
calibration.
Section~\ref{sec:license} is the philosophical core: what a calibrated
match licenses and what it does not.
Section~\ref{sec:negative} states the fixed list of negative claims,
and Sec.~\ref{sec:repro} maps every central number to its harness,
result file, and verification gate.

\subsection{Per-sample scale exponents}
\label{sec:exponents}

The simulator is dimensionless.
A \emph{scale declaration} attaches to each sample a triple of
exponents $\{L,T,M\}$ meaning that one simulation length unit is
$10^{L}$\,m, one time unit is $10^{T}$\,s, and one mass unit is
$10^{M}$\,kg.
The declaration is display-only: no equation of motion reads it, and
two samples that differ only in their exponents run bit-identically.
Its role is to make the numbers on the screen and in the harness
readable as SI quantities, and---critically---to decide whether the
\emph{constants} of the model can simultaneously be given their real
values.

The gravitational constant transforms as
\begin{equation}
  G_{\mathrm{SI}} = G_{\mathrm{sim}}\times10^{k},
  \qquad k = 3L-2T-M ,
  \label{eq:Gk}
\end{equation}
so a sample can carry the SI gravitational constant~\cite{CODATA2022}
exactly if $G_{\mathrm{sim}}=6.674\times10^{-11}\times10^{-k}$.
The three reality-calibration samples of this paper all satisfy
$k=-11$, which is why all three run with the same $G_{\mathrm{sim}}=
6.674$ (Table~\ref{tab:scales}).
This is the \emph{real-$G$ convention}.

The speed of light needs a separate treatment, because the dimensional
combination $c_{\mathrm{dim}}=c_0\times10^{L-T}$ does not equal the SI
speed of light for any of the model's standard scale tiers.
The \emph{real-$c$ convention} therefore chooses $c_0$ per sample so
that the dimensional conversion is exact:
$c_{0}^{*}=3\times10^{8}\times10^{-(L-T)}$.
For all three samples here $L-T=4$, giving
$c_0=3\times10^{4}$.
The consequence is stated once and used throughout: with the real-$G$
and real-$c$ conventions in force simultaneously, \emph{every
dimensionless invariant of the sample is real}---both $GM/(c^{2}a)$
and $v/c$---so the model's ``exaggeration factor'' relative to nature
is unity rather than the $10^{6}$ that the earlier $c_0=30$
visualization convention carried.

\subsection{The correspondence lock
  \texorpdfstring{$\kappa=G/c_0^{2}$}{kappa = G/c0\textasciicircum 2}}
\label{sec:physlock}

DFM reads clocks and light from the single dimensionless potential
$\psi = W\kappa$, where $\kappa$ (the parameter \texttt{kappaT}) is
the spacetime coefficient and $W$ the determinacy field; the earlier
papers wrote the same quantity as $\psi=W/K_t$ with $K_t=1/\kappa$.
The two forms are equivalent, and $\kappa$ is now the canonical
editing, declaration, and storage key throughout the
implementation, with $K_t$ retained only as a backward-compatible
input.\footnote{The engine's internal canonicalization to $\kappa$
(with $\psi=W\kappa$ evaluated as a multiplication rather than a
division) leaves the matter sector bit-identical---E4 gravity,
E6$'$ dragging and the E12 geodesic term do not read $\kappa$---and
perturbs only clocks, optics, and heads-up displays at the ULP level,
far inside every analytic-comparison tolerance.}
$\kappa$ carries dimensions of length per mass, the same as $G/c^{2}$,
so $\kappa m$ is a gravitational length.

The \emph{correspondence lock} (\texttt{physLock}) is the declaration
\begin{equation}
  \kappa = \frac{G}{c_{\mathrm{light}}^{2}} ,
  \label{eq:lock}
\end{equation}
which places the clock-and-light sector in the same weak-field
correspondence with general relativity that Paper~1 established for
its benchmark preset.
With $G=6.674$ and $c_0=3\times10^{4}$ the lock gives
$\kappa=7.4156\times10^{-9}$, equivalently $K_t=1.3485\times10^{8}$;
all six samples of this paper declare exactly this value, and a
verification gate asserts that the lock is satisfied at the moment
each harness configuration is built.
An honest bookkeeping note belongs here: because $c_0$ alone is carried
by a display convention rather than by the dimensional conversion,
multiplying displayed constants together to derive a third displayed
quantity is not a legitimate operation, and the project's canon says
so explicitly; the dimensional-conversion system
($G$, $\kappa$, $g_x/g_y$, $\varepsilon$, $D_0$) is internally
consistent on its own.

\subsection{The dragging profile and its two parameters}
\label{sec:profile}

The quantities calibrated in Secs.~\ref{sec:p3}--\ref{sec:p4} live in
the model's Machian sector, so we restate the three equations they
involve.
The determinacy weight of a source $j$ at distance $d_j$, the field,
and the local frame velocity are
\begin{align}
  w_j &= \frac{m_j}{\sqrt{d_j^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}}},
  \qquad W = D_0+\sum_j w_j,
  \label{eq:W}\\
  \bm u(\bm r) &= \frac{1}{W(\bm r)}\sum_j w_j
   \Bigl[\bm v_j+\omega_j(d_j)\,\hat z\times(\bm r-\bm r_j)\Bigr],
  \label{eq:u}
\end{align}
with the \emph{spin term} of a source of radius $R_j$ and spin $s_j$,
\begin{equation}
  \omega_j(d) = s_j\left(\frac{R_j}{R_j+d}\right)^{q}.
  \label{eq:spinterm}
\end{equation}
Dragging transports a particle through the change of its own local
frame, $\Delta\bm v_i = k_{\mathrm{Frame}}\,[\,\bm u_i(\text{now})-
\bm u_i(\text{prev})\,]$, with the particle's own contribution
excluded from $\bm u_i$, and with the reaction distributed back to the
sources in proportion to their weights $\varphi_{ij}=w_j/W$ (the
background share $\varphi_{\mathrm{bg}}=D_0/W$ going to an infinite
reservoir that the ledger tracks).

Two structural features of Eqs.~(\ref{eq:W})--(\ref{eq:spinterm})
organize this paper.
First, $\bm u$ has a \emph{motion} part (the $\bm v_j$ term) and a
\emph{spin} part (the $\omega_j$ term), and they calibrate
independently: the motion part is controlled by the background $D_0$
through the frame weight
\begin{equation}
  \chi = \frac{w}{D_0+w},
  \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
while the spin part is controlled by the geometric falloff exponent
$q$.
Second, $D_0$ has dimensions (mass over length), so the numerical value
that calibrates a system carries that system's scale---the fact that
Sec.~\ref{sec:p2} turns into a statement about stability and
Sec.~\ref{sec:license} into a limitation.

The solar-system row's choice $q=3$ with $D_0=0.1$ was made so that
the effective falloff index
\begin{equation}
  n(d) = q+\frac{D_0}{D_0+w(d)}
  \label{eq:neff}
\end{equation}
equals $3$---the Lense--Thirring distance
law~\cite{LenseThirring1918}---throughout the

Equation~(\ref{eq:neff}) is the model's built-in crossover: $n\to q$
where the central source dominates and $n\to q+1$ where the background
does, without introducing any radius-dependent $q(r)$.
That design decision---to express ``different values suit the center and
the outskirts'' through the pair $(q,D_0)$ rather than through a new
radial model---was taken before the calibrations reported here and is
not revisited.

\subsection{Two further conventions that the measurements forced}
\label{sec:forced}

Two settings in the sample declarations are not aesthetic choices but
consequences of measurements, and are stated here so that later
sections can refer to them.

\paragraph*{Mass floor.}
The effective-mass floor that protects the field evaluation from
vanishing masses was lowered to $10^{-6}$ by declaration
(\texttt{massFloor}) in the reality-calibration samples.
With the default floor, the Moon's real mass in the Earth--Moon
exponent system sits below the floor, and the floor's clamping
inflates the Earth-side attraction by a factor $1.36$---a measured
distortion of a real orbit by a numerical guard, removed by the
declaration.

\paragraph*{Softening.}
The Plummer softening length $\varepsilon$ is the source of an honest
failure documented in Sec.~\ref{sec:p1soft}, and the
reality-calibration samples run with $\varepsilon=0.05$ (Mercury,
Saturn) or $\varepsilon=0.1$ (Earth--Moon) instead of the default
$\varepsilon=2$ for this reason.

\section{P1: \texorpdfstring{43$''$}{43 arcsec}/century by direct
  dynamics}
\label{sec:p1}

\subsection{Why this was twice out of scope}
\label{sec:p1history}

Mercury's perihelion advance is the model's most exposed test, because
the geodesic option E12 of Paper~1 reproduces the $+42.98''$/century
main term~\cite{Clemence1947,Park2017,Will2014} \emph{at the level of
the equations}, with $\beta=\gamma=1$
and no additional free coefficient, while the base dynamics
E1$'$--E11 provably does not (Paper~1, negative claim~5).
Formula-level agreement, however, is not the same as measuring the
advance in a running orbit, and until the conventions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:conv} were in place the running measurement was
impossible for two independent reasons.

The first was range.
Mercury's dimensionless depth is
$d\equiv GM/(c^{2}a)=2.55\times10^{-8}$.
Under the earlier visualization convention $c_0=30$, realizing that
depth requires a central mass $M=5.2\times10^{-4}$, outside the
admissible mass range of the engine; under the real-$c$ convention
$c_0=3\times10^{4}$ the same depth is realized at $M=515.7$, comfortably
inside it.
This quantitative statement is the reason the real-$c$ convention was
adopted at all.

The second was numerical representation.
With the real invariants in range, the 1PN advance per orbit is
$5\times10^{-7}$\,rad, and in single-precision state the measurement
returned only $23$--$26\%$ of the analytic value: the per-step velocity
increments were being rounded away systematically, and the orbital
state itself performed a rounding random walk with a floor of
$\approx10^{-6}$\,rad/orbit over $3000$ orbits.
The remedy, adopted as an opt-in physics key
(\texttt{stateCarry:"double"}), promotes the acceleration accumulators
and the orbital state $x,y,v_x,v_y$ to double precision and makes the
velocity kick and the uniform external field compensated (Kahan) sums.
The default path is bit-identical, so no previously published number
moves.

\subsection{The invariant ladder}
\label{sec:p1ladder}

The measurement is a ladder in depth rather than a single run, so that
the approach to the real invariant can be seen rather than asserted.
Each rung fixes $a=150$, $e=0.206$ and a pinned primary declared as a
1PN source, chooses $M$ so that $GM=d\,c^{2}a$, and measures the
apsidal drift with a \emph{paired-run differential estimator}: the same
configuration is integrated twice, once with the 1PN term on
($\lambda_{PN}=1$) and once with it off ($\lambda_{PN}=0$), and the
difference of the two drifts is the reported quantity.
The estimator is a per-orbit boxcar (window averages over one Kepler
period, then least squares on the sequence of window means), which
removes the window-edge bias of the periodic component.
The comparison is with the weak-field expression
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\varpi_{1\mathrm{PN}} = \frac{6\pi\,d}{1-e^{2}} .
  \label{eq:1pn}
\end{equation}

  \Delta\varpi = 0.1037''/\text{orbit} = 43.0''/\text{century},

\end{equation}
against the observed $42.98''$/century~\cite{Park2017}---agreement to
better than $0.2\%$.
The claim this licenses is narrow and worth stating precisely: the
advance is now obtained \emph{from the dynamics of a running orbit
carrying Mercury's real invariants}, not from evaluating a formula and
overlaying it, and not from an exaggerated analogue.
It does not add evidence for the model beyond what Paper~1's
formula-level checks already established, because the equation that the
run integrates is the same one those checks verified; what it removes
is the caveat that the equation could not actually be run at the real
depth.

state it once for use throughout: the $43''$/century entry is a
\emph{dynamical verification---a benchmark---of the 1PN implementation},
obtained with the paired-run differential estimator of
Sec.~\ref{sec:p1ladder}.
It is neither a prediction nor a calibration: nothing was adjusted to
produce it (Sec.~\ref{sec:audit}), and the number it reproduces is one
that the implemented equation was already known analytically to carry.
The corresponding labels for the other headline numbers are:
the lunar apsidal cycle of Sec.~\ref{sec:p3} is a \emph{calibration of
two knobs against two observables}---a zero-degree-of-freedom two-point
interpolation, not a fit with residual freedom; and the loop gain $g$ of
Sec.~\ref{sec:p2} is an \emph{empirical organizing variable} rather than
a control parameter that was set.

The in-application sample carries the same construction with all
orbital elements at their observed values~\cite{Prsa2016,NASAFact}:
solar mass $1988.5$

$5.897643$ ($58.98$\,km/s), giving a measured orbital period of
$87.974$\,d against the observed $87.969$\,d.

\subsection{The honest failure: softening produces retrograde
  precession}
\label{sec:p1soft}

The subtraction in Sec.~\ref{sec:p1ladder}---measuring with the 1PN
term on and off and differencing---is not a refinement.
It is required, and the reason is a failure worth reporting in full.

Plummer softening replaces $1/d^{2}$ attraction by
$d/(d^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{3/2}$, which is not an inverse-square law,
so a softened Kepler orbit does not close: it precesses
\emph{retrograde} by
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\varpi_{\varepsilon} \simeq -3\pi\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{a}
  \right)^{2}
  \label{eq:soft}
\end{equation}
per orbit, independently of the time step.
On the ladder configuration ($a=150$, $\varepsilon=2$) this is
$-1.68\times10^{-3}$\,rad/orbit, and the measured
$\lambda_{PN}=0$ residual is $-1.8\times10^{-3}$\,rad/orbit---i.e.\ the
residual is quantitatively the softening precession, and it is
\emph{three to four orders of magnitude larger} than the 1PN signal
being sought.
Because Eq.~(\ref{eq:soft}) does not involve $\lambda_{PN}$, the
difference of the two runs cancels it exactly; that is why the
subtraction method is the method of record and why the raw
precession accumulator is not.

The same effect had a visible consequence in the application.
The Mercury sample originally ran at the default $\varepsilon=2$ with
$a=579.09$, where Eq.~(\ref{eq:soft}) gives
$-1.2\times10^{-4}$\,rad/orbit ($\approx24.7''$/orbit)---about $240$
times the 1PN advance of $5.03\times10^{-7}$\,rad/orbit---so the
on-screen precession accumulator was displaying, essentially, the
softening artifact.
% Note: earlier canon entries recorded this ratio as "24x"; that was a
% transcription slip (confusing the 24.7''/orbit magnitude with the
% ratio) and was corrected to ~240x in docs/PHYSICS.md at wave 129.
The remedy was to declare $\varepsilon=0.05$ for that sample (and
$\varepsilon=0.1$ for the Earth--Moon samples), and to record
explicitly that even then the raw accumulator retains an
integrator-level numerical floor, so that the calibrated
$43''$/century figure of record is the subtraction measurement of
Table~\ref{tab:ladder} and the value shown on the sample's observation
card---not the raw readout.
We report this because it is exactly the kind of artifact that a toy
model can present as a physical result if nobody differences it away.

\section{P2: stability is unit-system dependent}
\label{sec:p2}

\subsection{The loop gain}
\label{sec:p2gain}

A free two-body system with full dragging ($k_{\mathrm{Frame}}=1$)
frequently collapses: the eccentricity runs to unity within a few
orbits.
Systematic dose measurements identify an \emph{empirical organizing
variable}---a quantity along which the measured outcomes order
themselves, not a knob that was set and not a proven control
parameter---in the form of a \emph{loop gain} built from the two
bodies' frame weights,
\begin{equation}
  g = \frac{w_{\mathrm{sat}}}{D_0+w_{\mathrm{sat}}}\cdot
      \frac{w_{M}}{D_0+w_{M}}
    = \chi_{\mathrm{sat}}\,\chi_{M},
  \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
which is the product of the satellite's share of the primary's local
frame and the primary's share of the satellite's.
Dragging transports each body through the change of the other's frame;
Eq.~(\ref{eq:gain}) is the round-trip factor of that mutual transport,
and when it is too large the loop is a positive feedback.

Table~\ref{tab:gain} and Fig.~\ref{fig:gain} give the measured dose
response along two routes.
The gain that bounds stability in these runs lies between the largest
stable value $4.1\times10^{-3}$ and the smallest unstable one
$8.9\times10^{-3}$, but the boundary is route-dependent: reducing the
gain by shrinking the satellite's weight alone leaves the system
unstable at $g=8.9\times10^{-3}$, while reducing it by raising the
background is stable at $g=4.1\times10^{-3}$.
The asymmetry is itself informative---the primary-side factor
$\chi_M\approx0.94$ persists under a satellite-only correction, so one
factor of Eq.~(\ref{eq:gain}) never comes down.

\subsection{Why this makes stability a property of the unit system}
\label{sec:p2units}

The weights entering Eq.~(\ref{eq:gain}) are $w=m/\sqrt{d^{2}+
\varepsilon^{2}}$, which is \emph{dimensional}: it carries mass over
length.
The background $D_0$ carries the same dimensions.
Consequently the ratio $\chi=w/(D_0+w)$---and with it the loop
gain---depends on the numerical values of $m$ and $d$, that is, on the
declared scale exponents, even when the physical system being
represented is identical.

The effect is not marginal.
The same Earth--Moon system, declared under the exponent set used
before the reality-calibration samples were rebuilt, gives
$g\approx32\%$ and collapses immediately (a positive control reaches
$106\%$ radial excursion within $0.008$ of an orbit); declared under
the exponent set of Table~\ref{tab:scales} it gives
$g\approx2.9\times10^{-6}$ and survives six orbits with the excursion
equal to the natural eccentricity ($2e$) and a period residual of
$0.05\%$.
A change of display-only exponents cannot change physics---and it does
not: what it changes is which \emph{numerical} configuration the
declaration produces, and the model's dragging sector is sensitive to
that number because $D_0$ is not dimensionless.

This is the root of every subtlety about $D_0$ in the rest of the
paper, and we state it early because it determines what
Sec.~\ref{sec:p4}'s ``one common value'' can and cannot mean.

\subsection{Routes that work, and routes that were tested and failed}
\label{sec:p2routes}

The systematic search recorded three effective routes and four
ineffective ones.
The effective routes are: (i)~a $GM$-preserving mass rescaling
$(G,m)\to(kG,m/k)$, which leaves orbits, periods and the 1PN term
exactly invariant while dividing \emph{both} factors of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:gain}) by $k$, with a monotone dose from
$k=2$ to $k=50$ taking the radial excursion from $100\%$ to $10\%$;
(ii)~raising the background $D_0$, as in Table~\ref{tab:gain}; and
(iii)~pinning the primary, which removes one factor of the loop
entirely at the cost of declaring an open system.
A reduced-mass pair-reaction rule is a partial route on its own
($49\%$ excursion) and reaches $15.7\%$ in combination with the
rescaling.

The ineffective routes are reported with the same weight.
Damping through the thermal and spin channels---spin diffusion,
radiative cooling, spin repulsion, tangential friction---collapses in
every case: the instability lives in the frame field $\bm u$, not in
the spin bookkeeping, so no thermal channel touches it.
Dosing the falloff exponent $q$ over $\{2,3,4\}$ likewise collapses.
Correcting the satellite mass alone fails for the structural reason
given above.
Reporting these is not modesty; it is what makes the organizing role of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:gain}) falsifiable, since a channel that damped the
collapse without touching $g$ would show that the outcomes do not in
fact order themselves along $g$.

The three effective routes are codified as the stabilization procedure
for reality-calibration samples, in priority order: pin the primary
(and declare the system open); or raise $D_0$ to $\gtrsim2$ (permitted
only where dragging is not being calibrated, since $D_0$ also sets the
crossover radius $r_t\approx m/D_0$); or set $k_{\mathrm{Frame}}=0$
(permitted only where transport is not being claimed).
The $k_{\mathrm{Frame}}=0$ route is what the three non-dragging
reality-calibration samples use, and it is the reason those samples
make no dragging claim at all.

\section{P3: the lunar apsidal cycle from motion dragging}
\label{sec:p3}

\subsection{The origin of the drag precession}
\label{sec:p3origin}

With $k_{\mathrm{Frame}}=1$ the Earth--Moon system precesses.
Decomposition measurements settle where the precession comes from:
it is unchanged when both spins are set to zero, and unchanged when
the 1PN coefficient $\lambda_{PN}$ is switched between $0$ and $1$.
It is therefore a \emph{motion-dragging} effect---the $\bm v_j$ term of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:u}), i.e.\ the mutual orbital motion entering each body's
local frame---and not a spin effect and not a post-Newtonian one.

Its magnitude scales as the frame weight $\chi=w/(D_0+w)$ of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:chi}): at $D_0=0.1$ the measured advance is
$+2.7\times10^{-3}$\,rad/orbit, and raising $D_0$ to $1$ reduces it by
a factor $10.7$.
This makes $D_0$ a calibration knob for a precession rate, which is the
observation that P3 exploits.

\subsection{A two-body system with an apsidal cycle}
\label{sec:p3fit}

The real Moon's line of apsides completes a revolution in $8.85$
years, about $118$ sidereal
months~\cite{ExplSup2013,NASAFact}, i.e.\ $\Delta\varpi\approx0.0531$
rad per orbit.
A pure two-body Kepler orbit cannot do this: the ellipse closes.
The DFM Earth--Moon system with dragging on can, and a joint fit of two
quantities against two observables fixes it:
\begin{equation}
  D_0 = 0.006, \qquad
  f_{v} = 0.9968 \;(\text{initial speed } -0.32\%),
  \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
after which the measured sidereal month is $27.33$\,d against the
observed $27.3217$\,d~\cite{NASAFact} (a residual of $+0.03$ to
$+0.04\%$) and the
measured apsidal advance reproduces the $8.85$-year cycle, with an
all-window ratio of $0.99$ under the wave-120 configuration.
The two knobs are named and counted: $D_0$ sets the precession rate,
the initial-speed factor restores the period that $D_0$ perturbs, and
nothing else is adjusted---the masses, radii, spins, semi-major axis
and eccentricity are the observed values of Table~\ref{tab:scales}.
The epistemic label follows from that count and is used consistently
below: this is a \emph{calibration of two knobs against two
observables}, i.e.\ a two-point interpolation with zero degrees of
freedom left over.
Two adjusted quantities matched to two measured quantities cannot fail
to agree, so the agreement itself carries no evidential weight; what
carries content is that the two knobs are of the stated kind, that no
third one exists, and that the resulting configuration then went on to
behave in ways nobody fitted (Secs.~\ref{sec:p4joint}
and~\ref{sec:jupiter}).

Two honest qualifications attach to the number.
First, the apsidal ratio is \emph{window dependent}: the accumulator
oscillates strongly within each orbit---locally it even runs
retrograde---and averaging over different windows moves the ratio by
up to $\pm15\%$.
The quoted value is the per-orbit net over the full run, and the
convention has to be stated with the number, because no single
``the'' precession rate exists in this system at sub-orbit resolution.
Second, the fit was performed at $q=3$; Sec.~\ref{sec:p4} shows that
part of the residual was a spin-term contamination that a later
correction removes, improving the ratio to $0.9991$.

\subsection{What this is not}
\label{sec:p3not}

The real lunar apsidal cycle is driven principally by solar
perturbation: the Sun is a third body, and the $8.85$-year period is a
resonant consequence of its tidal forcing of the Earth--Moon orbit.
The DFM system contains two bodies and no Sun.
Whatever it is doing, it is not doing what nature does.

We therefore make exactly this claim and no other: \emph{a two-body
relational system with strong dragging, calibrated with one background
value and one initial-speed correction, matches two lunar observables
simultaneously---and it does so through a mechanism (motion dragging)
that is not the mechanism operating in the sky.}
The value of the result is that it is a clean, fully auditable example
of the general hazard: an observable that ``a two-body model cannot
possibly produce'' turns out to be producible, at the observed value,
by a different mechanism with a small number of knobs.
A reader who took the agreement as evidence for the mechanism would be
wrong, and the model itself supplies the disproof by containing no
third body.

A later measurement sharpens \emph{which} channel carries the match,
and the answer separates it further from the Lense--Thirring effect
that a reader might have in mind.
Running the same Earth-centred configuration with the primary pinned
and a mass-floor test particle as the probe---which leaves only the
spin-dragging channel---produces no $8.85$-year cycle at all: the
apsidal period at the Moon's radius comes out longer than
$10^{11}$ years.
Restoring the probe's mass restores the cycle, and the drag precession
scales with that mass: $7.9\times10^{-6}$\,rad/orbit at mass-floor
probe mass, $5.8\times10^{-4}$ at one hundredth of the Moon's mass,
and $5.2\times10^{-2}$ at the Moon's own mass, i.e.\ apsidal periods
of $5.5\times10^{4}$, $751$ and $8.25$ years respectively (harness
\texttt{tests/exp-qlockradial.mjs}, recorded in
\texttt{tests/out/qlockradial-results.json}).
The $8.85$-year calibration is therefore carried by the primary's own
motion about the common barycentre, whereas the channel whose far-field
amplitude the qLock rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:qlock} declares to be
LT-class is the spin channel (Fig.~\ref{fig:profile}).
The two channels are separately measurable and are set by different
things, so a reader who took the lunar agreement as evidence about
frame dragging of the Lense--Thirring kind would be crossing between
them.

This is reported as a section, not a footnote, because its position in
the paper is the point.

\section{P4: from a joint correction to a derived exponent}
\label{sec:p4}

\subsection{The failure that motivated it}
\label{sec:p4fail}

The calibration of Sec.~\ref{sec:p3} did not transfer.
Applying $k_{\mathrm{Frame}}=1$ to the Mercury system with the Sun's
% TODO(ref-verify): 25.05 d -- see the note at Table~\ref{tab:scales}.
real rotation ($25.05$\,d, equatorial sidereal) and the solar-system
profile $q=3$,
$D_0=0.1$ produces a \emph{retrograde} drag precession of
$-7.3\times10^{-5}$\,rad/orbit, i.e.\ $-1.5\times10^{4}\,''$/century:
three orders of magnitude away from the observed $+43''$/century, and
of the wrong sign.
Setting the Sun's spin to zero recovers the $k_{\mathrm{Frame}}=0$
result exactly, isolating the spin term of Eq.~(\ref{eq:spinterm}) as
the culprit.

The first repair was to apply P3's knob: since drag precession scales
as $\chi=w/(D_0+w)$, raising $D_0$ suppresses it, and $D_0=10^{4}$
restores $43''$/century with dragging on.
That worked and was, on its own terms, a failure.
The Earth--Moon calibration needed $D_0=0.006$; the Mercury
calibration needed $D_0=10^{4}$; the two differ by seven orders of
magnitude, and the honest conclusion recorded at the time was that
$D_0$ is a per-system calibration value and not a universal constant of
the model---a direct consequence of its dimensionality
(Sec.~\ref{sec:p2units}).
That interim answer is the failure this section exists to resolve.

\subsection{Stage (i): the joint correction
  \texorpdfstring{$(D_0,q)=(0.006,5)$}{(D0,q) = (0.006,5)}}
\label{sec:p4joint}

The resolution came from the decomposition rather than from a finer
fit.
The two systems are calibrated by \emph{different terms} of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:u}): the Earth--Moon precession is a motion-term effect
(Sec.~\ref{sec:p3origin}), while the Mercury retrograde precession is a
spin-term effect, because a pinned Sun has $\bm v=0$ and therefore
contributes to $\bm u$ only through $\omega(d)$.
The motion term is controlled by $D_0$; the spin term is controlled by
$q$.
So the right repair is not to suppress everything with $D_0$ but to
attenuate the spin term geometrically, leaving the motion-term
calibration untouched.

Raising $q$ multiplies the spin term at orbital distance $a$ by the
suppression factor $(R/(R+a))^{q-3}$ relative to $q=3$, so the exponent
required to bring a measured spin-term amplitude $|A_3|$ below a
tolerance $\epsilon$ is
\begin{equation}
  q^{*} = 3+\frac{\ln(|A_3|/\epsilon)}{\ln\bigl((R+a)/R\bigr)} .
  \label{eq:qstar1}
\end{equation}
With the measured $q=3$ amplitude and the tolerance set at one eighth
of the 1PN advance, Eq.~(\ref{eq:qstar1}) gave $q^{*}=4.59$ for
Mercury, and $q=5$ was adopted.
At $q=5$ the residual drag is $-5.3\times10^{-8}$\,rad/orbit, one tenth
of the 1PN term, so $43''$/century holds with dragging on;
$D_0$ returns to the Earth--Moon value $0.006$; and, unexpectedly, the
Earth--Moon calibration \emph{improves}, its apsidal ratio moving from
$0.988$ to $0.999$ as the Earth's own spin-term contamination is
attenuated out of a fit that had silently absorbed it.
One pair of constants, $(D_0,q)=(0.006,5)$, thus satisfied both systems
at once, and the seven-order tension dissolved.

The honest reading of stage (i) is that it traded one problem for a
smaller one.
The value $q=5$ was derived from Mercury's geometry through
Eq.~(\ref{eq:qstar1}) and then found to be harmless for the
Earth--Moon system---which is a coincidence of scales, not a
principle---and Eq.~(\ref{eq:qstar1}) itself contains a measured
amplitude $|A_3|$ and a chosen tolerance $\epsilon$, so $q$ remained a
calibration knob, merely one with a good story.

\subsection{Stage (ii): qLock, the derivation rule}
\label{sec:qlock}

The purification removes the knob, and it is worth being exact about
what the resulting rule does and does not assert, because the natural
short description of it (``it matches Lense--Thirring at the reference
orbit'') is stronger than the algebra supports.

Take the spin term of Eq.~(\ref{eq:spinterm}) and the Lense--Thirring
rate of a uniformly rotating sphere, $\Omega_{LT}=2GJ/(c_0^{2}r^{3})$
with $J=\tfrac{2}{5}MR^{2}s$, i.e.
$\Omega_{LT}=GMR^{2}s/(1.25\,c_0^{2}r^{3})$.
At $q=3$ and \emph{far from the source} ($d\gg R$) the bare spin term
tends to $sR^{3}/d^{3}$, so the ratio of the two tends to a constant in
which the orbit $d$ and the spin $s$ both cancel:
\begin{equation}
  X \;=\; \lim_{d\gg R}\frac{\omega_{q=3}(d)}{\Omega_{LT}(d)}
    \;=\; \frac{1.25\,c_0^{2}R}{GM},
  \label{eq:X}
\end{equation}
a pure function of the source's radius, mass, and the model's speed of
light.
Attenuating that far-field excess by exactly $X$ costs a geometric
factor $(R/(R+a))^{q-3}$ evaluated at a declared reference orbit $a$,
so the exponent is computable in closed form from known quantities:
\begin{equation}
  q^{*} = 3+\frac{\ln X}{\ln\bigl((R+a)/R\bigr)}
        = 3+\frac{\ln\bigl(1.25\,c_0^{2}R/GM\bigr)}
                 {\ln\bigl((R+a)/R\bigr)} .
  \label{eq:qlock}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:qlock}) contains no measured amplitude and no chosen
tolerance.
The reference orbit $a$ is declared as the median initial distance of
the free bodies from the dominating source---a stated convention, and
the only remaining discretion in the rule.

\paragraph*{What the rule declares, stated exactly.}
Equation~(\ref{eq:X}) is a \emph{far-field} statement, so qLock is a
\emph{declared convention fixing the spin term's far-field
($a\gg R$) amplitude to the Lense--Thirring scale}, and nothing
stronger.
Substituting the rule's own output back into the bare spin term and
comparing with $\Omega_{LT}$ \emph{at the reference orbit itself} gives,
exactly and with $s$ again cancelling,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\omega_{q^{*}}(a)}{\Omega_{LT}(a)}
    = \left(\frac{a}{R+a}\right)^{3},
  \label{eq:finrad}
\end{equation}
a pure geometric \emph{finite-radius factor} that tends to unity only as
$a/R\to\infty$.
Table~\ref{tab:finrad} evaluates it for the four systems of this paper.
It is close to unity where the reference orbit is far outside the
source (Mercury, the Moon) and markedly below unity where it is not
(the Saturn ring at $1.7\,R$, Io at $5.9\,R$).
Every statement in this paper about the spin term therefore reads
``LT-class amplitude by the far-field convention'', never ``agreement
with the Lense--Thirring rate'', and the same wording is used in
\texttt{docs/PHYSICS.md} and in the in-application sample claims.

\paragraph*{The convention that was not adopted.}
Forcing Eq.~(\ref{eq:finrad}) to unity is equally available in closed
form, and equally a convention rather than a derivation:
\begin{equation}
  q_{\mathrm{exact}} = q^{*}
    + \frac{3\ln\bigl(a/(R+a)\bigr)}{\ln\bigl((R+a)/R\bigr)} ,
  \label{eq:qexact}
\end{equation}
giving the right-hand column of Table~\ref{tab:finrad}.
We do not adopt it.
The reason is bookkeeping honesty rather than physics: the calibrated
state of record---the shared $D_0=0.006$, the measurements of
\texttt{tests/exp-kf1d.mjs}, and the Jovian hold-out of
Sec.~\ref{sec:jupiter}, which was pre-registered and run at
$q=12.30$---was established with Eq.~(\ref{eq:qlock}), and switching
conventions would require re-measuring all of it.
The correction that the present revision makes is the smaller and more
truthful one: keep the world line that was actually measured, and state
precisely what its rule declares.
Nothing in the paper's numbers depends on the choice, because
Sec.~\ref{sec:jupsens} measures the outcome under
$q_{\mathrm{exact}}$ as well and finds it indistinguishable from the
outcome under $q^{*}$.

Under this rule (\texttt{qLock}), $q$ ceases to be an editable
parameter in the declaring samples: it is recomputed on load, and the
interface greys the control out.
Table~\ref{tab:qlock} gives the derived exponents and the resulting
measurements.

\subsection{An entrainment profile, and a consistency remark}
\label{sec:mm}

Because Eq.~(\ref{eq:qlock}) fixes the exponent from the source's
surface geometry, it also fixes the shape of the entrainment profile
$\omega_{\mathrm{drag}}(d)$ relative to the Lense--Thirring rate
$\Omega_{LT}\propto d^{-3}$.
For the Saturn system the profile is strongly super-LT immediately
above the surface, crosses unity in the mid-ring region, and falls off
rapidly outward: at a surface height $h=0.5$ the ratio is $\times45$,
at $h\approx45$ it is $\times1$, and at Titan's distance it is far
below unity.

The shape of that profile can also be measured directly rather than
quoted, and for the Earth--Moon system it has been.
Figure~\ref{fig:profile} plots $\omega_{\mathrm{DFM}}/\Omega_{LT}$
from $1.5$ to $121$ Earth radii for the qLock exponent $q^{*}=8.25$
and for a flat control $q=3$, with the primary pinned and mass-floor
test particles as probes, so that the quantity plotted is the
\emph{spin} channel of the frame field alone; it is read from the
determinacy field itself rather than through an orbit integration
(harness \texttt{tests/exp-qlockradial.mjs}, recorded in
\texttt{tests/out/qlockradial-results.json}).
Three features of the shape are worth naming.
First, at the reference orbit the bare profile sits at $0.944$ of the
Lense--Thirring rate (with the saturation factor $\chi$ included,
$0.203$).
That value is not an accident of the run and not an agreement either:
it is Eq.~(\ref{eq:finrad}) evaluated at the sample's declared literal
$q=8.25$.
The analytic finite-radius factor at the unrounded $q^{*}=8.2487$ is
$0.9492$ (Table~\ref{tab:finrad}), and rounding $q$ to two decimals in
the declaration moves it to $0.9440$, which is what the engine's field
returns.
The measurement therefore confirms the algebra to four digits; it does
not test an agreement with nature, because the far-field convention of
Sec.~\ref{sec:qlock} never claimed one at this radius.
Second, outside that radius the profile falls with a log--log slope of
$-8.99$ in $\omega$, against $-3$ for Lense--Thirring and $-3.81$ for
the flat control---which is Cost~2 of Sec.~\ref{sec:cost2} in its
measured form, and the reason the ``$\times45$ / $\times1$ /
$\ll1$'' sequence quoted above for Saturn is steep.
Third, inward the ratio does not follow $\Omega_{LT}\propto r^{-3}$ to
infinity: $\chi=w/(D_0+w)$ approaches unity ($0.912$ at $1.5$ Earth
radii) and the DFM rate saturates while the reference rate diverges,
which is why the ratio reaches $2.9\times10^{6}$ there.

The saturation deserves a sentence of mechanism, because a reader
looking at Fig.~\ref{fig:profile} may reasonably ask what stops the
model's own rate from running away as $r$ decreases.
The frame rotation is the product of two factors,
$\omega_{\mathrm{DFM}}(r)=\chi(r)\,s\,(R/(R+r))^{q}$.
The geometric factor $(R/(R+r))^{q}$ is bounded above by unity and
tends to $1$, not to infinity, as $r\to0$: the profile is written in
terms of $R+r$ rather than $r$, so the source's own radius provides the
scale that keeps it finite.
The dilution factor $\chi=w/(D_0+w)$ is bounded above by unity as well,
and it is the one that saturates: as the local weight $w=m/d$ grows
towards the surface, $\chi$ rises towards $1$ and then \emph{stops},
reaching $0.912$ at $1.5$ Earth radii.
Both factors are therefore capped, and $\omega_{\mathrm{DFM}}$ is bounded
by the source's own spin rate $s$ everywhere---there is no divergence to
regulate.
What diverges is the \emph{reference}: $\Omega_{LT}\propto r^{-3}$ has
no such cap in the region plotted.
``Dragging is strongest nearest the surface'' is therefore a statement
about a bounded profile measured against a diverging reference, and the
next paragraph should be read with that in mind.

The structural remark that accompanies this---made in the project canon
and repeated here with its own qualification---is that ``dragging is
strongest nearest the surface'' is the configuration in which a
surface-mounted interferometer rides the co-rotating frame, and is
therefore consistent with the null result of the Michelson--Morley
experiment~\cite{MichelsonMorley1887}.
We record this as an observation about the \emph{shape} of a profile
and nothing more: it is not a derivation, not a quantitative comparison
with that experiment (no fringe shift, no velocity bound, and no
figure of merit of any kind is computed here), and not a mechanism
identification in the sense of Sec.~\ref{sec:definitions}.
It is placed in this subsection, after the results of P4 and outside
every claim the paper makes, because it is not one of them.

\subsection{A hypothesis tested and retired}
\label{sec:p4spin}

The investigation that produced the joint correction was directed to
examine the spin of the \emph{dragged} body as well as the dragging
one.
It was examined, over four decades: Mercury's own spin was set to zero,
to its real value, to $100\times$, and to $10^{4}\times$ its real
value, at both $q=3$ and the corrected exponent; the Moon's spin was
similarly scanned.
The precession is unchanged in every case.

The reason is structural rather than numerical: Eq.~(\ref{eq:u}) is
evaluated for particle $i$ with $i$ itself excluded from the sum, so a
body's own spin cannot enter its own local frame, and hence cannot
affect its own transport.
The hypothesis is therefore not merely unsupported by the measurement
but excluded by the construction.
We report it as a negative result with the same prominence as the
positive ones, since a reader is entitled to know which plausible
mechanisms were tested and closed.

With dragging off (JW1) the transcription reproduces the four sidereal

The honesty window (JW3) is the one that makes the rest meaningful:

The $q=3$ control---the exponent whose distance dependence is the

So the $q=3$ control does not blow the system up; it shifts the two
innermost periods out of a $1\%$ band while leaving the geometry intact.

The frame field itself was recorded rather than gated, and it
decomposes into two channels that point in opposite directions.
The full-system frame rotation at each moon is dominated by the
motion dragging of the \emph{other} moons and is not prograde; the
spin channel, isolated by running Jupiter with a single moon, is
prograde at all four radii and agrees with the analytic bare form to
better than $2\times10^{-7}$ in ratio, but it contributes less than
$10^{-5}$ of the total drag precession.
The drag differences themselves are read as magnitudes and signs, not
as precision numbers: halving the time step moves the periods by less
than $3\times10^{-5}$ in relative terms but moves the per-moon drag
difference by up to $23\%$, so only its order and sign are stable at
this resolution, and we quote it accordingly.
Repeating the main configuration reproduces the final state of all
four moons bit for bit.
The Laplace resonance was excluded from the pre-registered windows and
is recorded only: the measured period ratios are $2.0069$ and $2.0145$
against the observed $2.0073$ and $2.0147$, but the initial phases are
placed $90^{\circ}$ apart rather than at the resonant equilibrium, so
the resonant angle is not librating and no claim is made about it.

What this test licenses is again narrow, and narrower than it first
looks.
It is not a prediction of the Galilean periods: those are transcribed,
and JW1 shows the transcription working with the dragging term off.
What is genuinely out of sample is the \emph{survival} of an observed
four-body configuration under a dragging term whose exponent was
computed, not chosen---together with the failure of the same
configuration under the exponent that the Lense--Thirring distance law
would dictate.
A calibration that had absorbed the observations through a fitted
parameter would have had no reason to behave this way in a system it
never saw; that is the sense, and the only sense, in which the result
supports the rule.

\subsection{Why Io is the reference orbit, and how much that choice
  matters}
\label{sec:jupsens}

Equation~(\ref{eq:qlock}) needs one reference orbit, and a four-moon
system does not supply one canonically: the runtime rule takes the
median initial distance of the free bodies, which for a multi-body
system is an artifact of which bodies happen to be present.
The Jovian sample therefore declares the reference explicitly, and the
declaration is Io.
Three reasons were stated before the run.
Io is the moon closest to the dominating source, so it is where the spin
term is largest and the exponent is doing the most work; it has the
shortest period, so a window of fixed physical length contains the most
revolutions and the numerical floor of the drag differential is at its
best there (Sec.~\ref{sec:jupiter}); and choosing the innermost orbit
makes the other three genuinely held out, since a reference at, say,
Ganymede would sit between the bodies it is supposed to predict.

Whether the choice matters is a measurement rather than an argument, and
it was made in wave 141 (harness \texttt{tests/exp-jupiter.mjs} \S H,
recorded under \texttt{sensitivity} in
\texttt{tests/out/jupiter-results.json}).
The pre-registered windows JW1--JW5 and every number quoted above are
unchanged---the sensitivity block only adds runs---and the additions are
recorded, not gated.
Table~\ref{tab:jupsens} gives them.

Three readings follow, and the third is the one that qualifies the
result.

First, the reference-orbit choice barely propagates.
Moving $a$ by $\pm20\%$ moves the derived exponent by $+1.00$ and
$-0.70$ (from $12.30$ to $13.30$ and $11.60$), yet the four period
deviations are unchanged to every digit recorded, the radial excursions
differ by at most $1.5\times10^{-7}$ percentage points, and the drag
differential changes by at most $1.0\times10^{-5}$ in relative terms.
The declared reference is therefore a convention with a stated rationale
and almost no numerical consequence in this system.

Second, the same insensitivity holds across the whole family of large
exponents.
At $q=8$, $10$, $12.06$ and $14$---which span five orders of magnitude
in the bare spin-term amplitude at Io, from $2.5\times10^{3}$ down to
$2.4\times10^{-2}$ times $\Omega_{LT}$---the period deviations are again
identical to the recorded digits and the drag differential moves by at
most $0.7\%$ (largest at $q=8$).
In particular the $q_{\mathrm{exact}}$ convention of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:qexact}) is indistinguishable from the adopted one, which
is why Sec.~\ref{sec:qlock} can decline to switch conventions without
that decision touching any number.

Third---and this is the qualification---the insensitivity is itself the
reason the Jovian result is weaker than it looks, and it is consistent
with what Sec.~\ref{sec:jupiter} already reported about the channels.
The dynamics in this system is dominated by the moons' mutual motion
dragging; the spin channel, whose amplitude is what $q$ controls,
contributes less than $10^{-5}$ of the total drag precession.
An exponent anywhere in $[8,14]$ therefore produces the same orbits,
and what the hold-out actually discriminates is not a value of $q$ but
the difference between a strongly attenuated spin term and an
unattenuated one: $q=3$ shifts the two innermost periods by $1.46\%$ and
$1.09\%$, one to two orders of magnitude more than anything inside the
$q\geq8$ family.
The honest statement of the test is thus narrower than ``the derived
exponent is confirmed'': what is confirmed is that the rule lands
somewhere in a broad basin that works, while the exponent the
Lense--Thirring distance law would dictate does not.
Finally, the shared background is not a hidden knob for this system
either: halving or doubling $D_0$ leaves all four orbits retained and
all four periods inside $\pm1\%$, moving the largest period deviation
between $0.0486\%$ and $0.0514\%$.

\section{Ring-system scope}
\label{sec:ring}

The Saturn system is included for scope rather than for a new
mechanism, and its scope statement is the point of this short section.

The sample places Saturn's real mass ($56.834=5.6834\times10^{26}$\,kg),
real radius ($60.3=6.03\times10^{7}$\,m)~\cite{Prsa2016,NASAFact} and
real rotation ($0.016528$, i.e.\ a $10.56$-hour
day~\cite{Mankovich2019}) at the planetary exponents of
Table~\ref{tab:scales}, with the ring resolved as three bands at their
observed radii~\cite{NASAFact}---C $[74.658,92.0]$, B $[92.0,117.58]$,
A $[122.17,136.775]$, with the Cassini division at its real
position---populated by 40 representative grains each, plus the six
major moons from Mimas to Titan at their real orbital radii and
masses~\cite{Jacobson2006,NASAFact} with Keplerian initial speeds.
The calibrated observables are Keplerian: the C-ring inner edge orbits
at $2.25$ ($22.5$\,km/s) with a period of $5.78$\,h against the
\emph{derived Kepler period} $5.8$\,h---that is, the Keplerian period at
the catalogued inner-edge radius $74{,}658$\,km~\cite{NASAFact}, which
is a quantity derived from a catalogued radius and not itself a
catalogued observable---and Titan's period is $15.949$\,d against the
observed $15.945$\,d~\cite{Jacobson2006,NASAFact}.
The distinction is not pedantry: comparing against a derived Kepler
period tests the unit conversion and the integrator, whereas comparing
against Titan's tabulated period tests the transcription of an observed
number.
The same wording (``derived Kepler period'') is used on the sample's
observation card in the application, so that the two kinds of
comparison are not presented as one.
% wave 141: renamed from "observed 5.8 h" to "derived Kepler period",
% here and on the in-application observation card (QA claims.sync
% checked). If a directly measured value is ever wanted, cite a
% ring-occultation radius/kinematics source after verifying it.
All 120 ring particles are retained over the verification run.

The grain mass is a declared calibration and is stated as such: at
$10^{-5}$ ($10^{20}$\,kg) it is a representative value near the ring's
real total mass of $1.5\times10^{19}$\,kg~\cite{Iess2019},
whereas at $10^{-2}$
($10^{23}$\,kg) the grains' mutual gravity is some six orders of
magnitude stronger than the real ring's and the bands were measured to
scatter and accrete within a few orbits.
The choice is made to keep the orbits observational, and it means the
sample calibrates ring \emph{kinematics}, not ring dynamics.

What the sample does \emph{not} claim is more important than what it
does.
The dimensionless depth at the C ring is
$GM/(c^{2}r_C)=5.64\times10^{-9}$, shallower than Mercury's, and the
radial period is long enough that direct measurement of the 1PN effect
exceeds practical run times; the project's estimate---an extrapolation
from a depth scan in which the band residual roughly halves as the
depth is reduced, together with the linear convergence of the 1PN
ladder (ratios $1.001$--$1.002$ across all rungs)---is that at the real
invariants the band residual falls to the $0.01\%$ class, and the
$\pm2$--$6\%$ residuals measured at exaggerated depth are artifacts of
that exaggeration.
That is a prediction with a stated basis, not a measurement, and it is
labeled as such.
Likewise the Lense--Thirring sector stays out of scope: the
non-dragging Saturn sample runs at $k_{\mathrm{Frame}}=0$ by the
convention of Sec.~\ref{sec:p2routes}, and the dragging sample's
contribution is the qLock verification of Table~\ref{tab:qlock} and the
entrainment shape of Sec.~\ref{sec:mm}, neither of which is a
measurement of a real Lense--Thirring precession.
For reference, the real solar Lense--Thirring precession
($\sim0.002''$/century)~\cite{IorioEtAl2011} is $2\times10^{-4}$ of the
1PN term and lies
below this implementation's measurement floor, which is why the
dragging sector is verified by dose decomposition at exaggerated spin
rather than by direct comparison with the sky.

Finally, the failure that the calibration repairs is worth recording:
with dragging on at $q=3$, the real rotation of Saturn speeds the ring
up by $4.7\%$ and migrates it inward by $2.5\%$ over three orbits, and
turns the apsides of an eccentric test particle by $0.23$\,rad/orbit.
It is the qLock exponent that removes all three, and the visible
contrast between the two states is retained in the application as a
one-tap control so that a reader can see the uncalibrated behavior
rather than take our word for it.

\section{What calibration licenses---and what it does not}
\label{sec:license}

This section states the position that the preceding five sections were
written to support.

\subsection{The audit that a calibration must pass}
\label{sec:audit}

A calibrated agreement is a claim about a procedure, so the procedure
must be auditable in four respects, and we record them for the results
above.

\emph{What was adjusted.}
For P1: nothing---the conventions of Sec.~\ref{sec:conv} were fixed
before the measurement, the orbital elements are the observed ones, and
the ratio $1.002$ is an outcome.
For P3: two quantities ($D_0$ and the initial speed) against two
observables (the month and the apsidal cycle).
For P4 stage (i): one further quantity ($q$), derived from Mercury's
geometry and a chosen tolerance.
For P4 stage (ii): none---Eq.~(\ref{eq:qlock}) computes $q$ from
declared quantities, with the reference orbit convention as the only
remaining discretion.

\emph{What was predicted afterwards.}
The Earth--Moon apsidal ratio improved from $0.988$ to $0.999$ under a
correction derived from Mercury's geometry; the Saturn ring's spurious
apsidal drift collapsed by four orders of magnitude under an exponent
derived from Saturn's geometry.
Neither outcome was fitted.

\emph{What the number costs.}
Two costs are quantified below.

\emph{What mechanism produced it.}
Stated in each case, including the case where the mechanism is
demonstrably not nature's (Sec.~\ref{sec:p3not}).

\subsection{Cost 1: \texorpdfstring{$D_0$}{D0} is dimensional, so a
  calibration imports a scale}
\label{sec:cost1}

Because $D_0$ carries mass over length, the statement ``$D_0=0.006$ is
shared by all three calibrated systems'' needs its domain of validity
stated.
Inside the reality-calibration family it is stronger than it looks.
Imposing both conventions of Sec.~\ref{sec:exponents} on a sample---the
real-$G$ condition $3L-2T-M=-11$ and the real-$c$ condition $L-T=4$---
forces $M=3L-2T+11$ and $T=L-4$, hence
\begin{equation}
  M-L = 19
  \label{eq:ML}
\end{equation}
for \emph{every} sample that satisfies both.
Since $D_0$ has dimensions of mass over length, its unit is
$10^{M-L}=10^{19}$\,kg\,m$^{-1}$ in all of them, so the shared numeral
$0.006$ denotes the same physical background density in the Mercury
system and in the Earth--Moon and Saturn systems, despite their
different exponent triples (Table~\ref{tab:scales}).
The sharing is therefore a physical statement within that family, and
not an artifact of how the three samples happen to be declared.

Outside the family the statement has no content.
A sample that does not satisfy both conventions---the galactic
visualization presets, or the earlier planetary-tier declaration of the
Earth--Moon system, which satisfied $3L-2T-M=-8$ and failed the
correspondence lock---has a different unit for $D_0$, so the numeral
does not transfer, and Sec.~\ref{sec:p2units} shows that what moves
with it is not merely a label but the stability of the configuration.
The honest summary is thus narrower than ``a universal constant of the
model has been measured'' and wider than ``a coincidence of three
declarations'': one background density, in one family of samples
pinned to the real constants, satisfies three calibrations that
previously demanded values seven orders of magnitude apart.
A dimensionless reformulation of the background, which would remove the
qualification entirely, is not attempted here and is the natural next
question.
It is worth naming the shape such a reformulation would have to take,
without pretending that any of it has been done.
$D_0$ enters only through $\chi=w/(D_0+w)$ with $w=m/d$, so what the
theory would need is a declared reference weight $w_{\ast}$ with which
to form the dimensionless $\hat D_0=D_0/w_{\ast}$; the whole question is
where $w_{\ast}$ comes from.
Two candidate answers suggest themselves from the model's own
structure.
The Machian one takes $w_{\ast}$ from the summarized far universe---in
the box model of Paper~2 the box already supplies a total mass and a
horizon-like size, so $w_{\ast}\sim M_{\mathrm{tot}}/R_{H}$ is available
by construction and would make $\hat D_0$ a cosmological ratio rather
than a per-sample number.
The local one takes $w_{\ast}$ from the system itself, e.g.\
$w_{\ast}=M/a$ at the reference orbit, which would make $\hat D_0$
a property of the configuration and would predict that the calibrated
$\hat D_0$ differs between the Mercury, Earth--Moon and Saturn systems
in a stated way.
The two are distinguishable: the first predicts one $\hat D_0$ for all
samples, the second predicts a systematic spread.
Neither is implemented, neither is tested, and nothing in this paper
should be read as evidence for either; the point of stating them is
that the cost identified here has a definite next experiment attached to
it rather than an open-ended wish.

\subsection{Cost 2: qLock trades away the Lense--Thirring distance
  law}
\label{sec:cost2}

The solar-system profile was designed so that the effective falloff
index of Eq.~(\ref{eq:neff}) equals $3$, matching the
Lense--Thirring $\Omega\propto r^{-3}$, throughout the
central-dominated region.
The qLock exponents are $6.16$, $8.25$ and $21.8$.
The spin term therefore no longer obeys the Lense--Thirring distance
law at any radius.
It is worth being exact about what replaces it, because the natural way
to soften this cost---``except at the reference orbit, where the rule
forces agreement''---is not true.
Equation~(\ref{eq:qlock}) fixes the \emph{far-field} amplitude, so at
the reference orbit itself the bare spin term stands at the
finite-radius factor $(a/(R+a))^{3}$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:finrad})---$0.956$,
$0.949$ and $0.256$ for the three systems (Table~\ref{tab:finrad}), and
$0.203$ for the Earth--Moon system once the dilution $\chi$ is
included.
What the model buys is an \emph{amplitude convention}: the spin term is
declared to be of Lense--Thirring class in the far field, which is what
makes it small enough for the $43''$/century calibration to survive
under dragging.
What it pays is the radial profile of the dragging term, everywhere,
plus a computable offset at the reference orbit itself.
The non-dragging reality-calibration samples keep $q=3$, so the two
regimes remain separated and inspectable, but no reading of the
combined result can claim both.

\subsection{Cost 3: the window convention is part of the number}
\label{sec:cost3}

The lunar apsidal ratio depends on the averaging window at the $\pm15\%$
level, because the accumulator oscillates within each orbit strongly
enough to change sign locally.
The quoted ratios ($0.99$ at wave 120, $0.9991$ under qLock) are
per-orbit nets over the full run.
Quoting such a number without its window is not a small imprecision; it
is the difference between a measurement and a choice, and the same
caution applies to the raw on-screen accumulator discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:p1soft}.

\subsection{What follows}
\label{sec:follows}

Putting the three costs beside the four results: DFM can be driven to
real numbers, and when it is, it produces agreements of the kind
astronomers measure---one of them ($43''$/century) inherited from an
equation-level correspondence established in Paper~1, one of them (the
lunar apsidal cycle) produced by a mechanism that is not nature's, and
one of them (the qLock rule) obtained by removing a knob rather than
tuning it.
The instructive content is not that a toy model can be made to agree.
It is that agreement, in a system small enough to audit completely,
decomposes into parts with entirely different epistemic weight---and
that a model which does not perform that decomposition on itself will
present all of them as the same kind of success.

\section{Limitations and negative claims}
\label{sec:negative}

As in the companion papers, these statements are part of the thesis,
not disclaimers appended to it.

\subsection{Negative claim 1: no claim about real gravitation}
DFM is not proposed as a description of nature.
It remains a computational thought experiment: the study of a
counterfactual axiom system in which space is purely relational and
dragging is of order unity.
None of the calibrations above changes that status; they change only
which numbers the thought experiment can be asked for.

\subsection{Negative claim 2: calibrated match is not mechanism
  identification}
Every agreement reported here is a calibrated match.
The lunar apsidal cycle is the explicit case: the real driver is solar
perturbation, and the model reproduces the observable in a system with
no third body, through motion dragging.
No claim is made that any DFM mechanism is the mechanism operating in
the sky.

\subsection{Negative claim 3: \texorpdfstring{$D_0$}{D0} is not a
  universal constant}
The background $D_0$ carries dimensions.
Its shared value $0.006$ is a shared \emph{physical} background density
only within the family of samples that satisfy both the real-$G$ and
real-$c$ conventions, for which Eq.~(\ref{eq:ML}) fixes a common unit;
it does not transfer to samples outside that family, and the stability
of a configuration moves with the declaration
(Secs.~\ref{sec:p2units} and~\ref{sec:cost1}).

\subsection{Negative claim 4: qLock trades the Lense--Thirring
  distance law, and is a convention even where it agrees}
The derived exponents $q^{*}>3$ suppress the spin term geometrically
and thereby abandon the $\Omega\propto r^{-3}$ distance law at every
radius (Sec.~\ref{sec:cost2}).
The rule removes a calibration knob; it does not remove the trade.
Two further limits are stated here rather than left to be inferred.
First, qLock is a declared convention on the spin term's
\emph{far-field} amplitude, not a match to the Lense--Thirring rate at
the reference orbit: substituting the rule's own output back in leaves
the finite-radius factor $(a/(R+a))^{3}$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:finrad}),
which is $0.956$, $0.949$, $0.256$ and $0.625$ for the Mercury,
Earth--Moon, Saturn and Jovian reference orbits, and $0.203$ for the
Earth--Moon system once the background dilution $\chi$ is included.
Second, the alternative convention $q_{\mathrm{exact}}$ of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:qexact}) ($6.147$, $8.236$, $20.493$, $12.059$) would force
that factor to unity and is equally available; it was not adopted,
for the bookkeeping reason given in Sec.~\ref{sec:qlock}, and the
measured outcome is indistinguishable under the two
(Sec.~\ref{sec:jupsens}).
That a second convention exists, and that the model cannot tell them
apart, is part of the claim's limit and not a footnote to it.

\subsection{Negative claim 5: two dimensions, no Lorentz invariance, no
  causal propagation}
The model is two-dimensional, spin is a scalar, and the field $W$ and
frame $\bm u$ are set instantaneously by the global configuration.
No three-dimensional quantitative extrapolation is offered, and
phenomena requiring causal field propagation---gravitational radiation
foremost---remain outside the model in every mode.

\subsection{Negative claim 6: no solar-perturbation physics is
  simulated}
The Earth--Moon calibration contains two bodies.
The model implements no third-body perturbation theory, no evection or
variation terms, and no lunar theory of any kind.

\subsection{Negative claim 7: the ring result is Kepler-level only}
The Saturn calibration matches orbital periods and speeds.
The 1PN band residual at real invariants is an extrapolation, not a
measurement; the Lense--Thirring sector is out of scope; and the grain
mass is a declared calibration that makes the sample a kinematic, not a
dynamical, model of a ring.

\subsection{Negative claim 8: the entrainment/Michelson--Morley remark
  is a consistency observation}
That the entrainment profile is strongest near the surface is a
statement about a shape derived from Eq.~(\ref{eq:qlock}).
It is recorded as consistent with a surface interferometer riding the
co-rotating frame; it is not a derivation of, a quantitative comparison
with, or an explanation of the Michelson--Morley null result.

\subsection{Negative claim 9: quoted precession ratios are
  window-dependent}
The apsidal ratios are per-orbit nets over the stated run window and
move by up to $\pm15\%$ under other window conventions
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cost3}).
The raw on-screen accumulator additionally carries an
integrator-level numerical floor and is not the figure of record.

\subsection{Verification map}
\label{sec:map}

Each central number of this paper was produced once, by a named
measurement harness, and written to a committed result file; a separate
automated gate then \emph{pins} that recorded value in continuous
integration.
The two operations are different, and the map below keeps them in
different columns, because collapsing them---saying that each claim
``maps to an automated gate that runs in CI''---invites the reading that
CI re-measures the physics on every push.
It does not.
The measurement harnesses are deliberately outside the CI loop
(Sec.~\ref{sec:harnesses}); what CI re-runs is the comparison between
the committed numbers and either the application's declared state or
the regenerated figure data.

Three honesty notes attach to the map; the first two carry over from the
companion papers unchanged.
First, the gates are regression baselines whose tolerances were set
\emph{after} the first measurement (row four of
Table~\ref{tab:checkkinds}); they pin recorded values against drift and
can never be evidence for the values they pin.
Second, the harnesses drive the simulator itself, which guarantees that
the paper and the implementation agree but is not an independent proof
of the implementation; the analytic comparisons
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:1pn}, \ref{eq:soft}, \ref{eq:qlock},
\ref{eq:finrad}) are evaluated in the harness, outside the engine, and
it is those comparisons that carry the independent content.
Third, and specific to the figures: the figure generator runs no
simulation at all, so its gates are consistency checks between the
plotted values and the committed result files, not measurements---which
is why they are named regression/consistency gates rather than numeric
gates in the source.

\subsection{Code and data availability}
\label{sec:avail}

The simulator, the verification suite, the measurement harnesses, the
result files, and this manuscript are in the public repository
\url{https://github.com/tty-imamura/dfm-simulator} (code MIT,
documentation CC~BY~4.0).
The state of record for this paper is the tag \texttt{v1.41.0}, the
release that carries the Jupiter hold-out, the qLock radial audit and
the mechanism discrimination reported here, on top of the
observation-calibration program whose completion the v1.40 line
recorded---the real-$G$, real-$c$, \texttt{pnSource} and
\texttt{stateCarry} conventions, the reality-calibration sample set,
and the purification of the kF1 joint correction into
$D_0=0.006$ plus qLock with no remaining calibration knob.
% TODO(release): Zenodo DOI only --- the annotated tag is settled
% (v1.41.0); the DOI is minted at submission time, in the form used by
% Papers 1--2 (Paper 1: doi:10.5281/zenodo.21454189).
The environment is Node.js~$\geq$22 with a Playwright-driven Chromium
for the harnesses and TeX~Live for the manuscript;
\texttt{npm ci \&\& npm test} reproduces the verification suite and
\texttt{node tools/gen-figures3.mjs} regenerates every figure of this
paper.
The figure generator runs no simulation: it reads the committed result
files listed in Sec.~\ref{sec:harnesses}, redraws
Figs.~\ref{fig:ladder}--\ref{fig:jupiter} from them, writes the
plotted values beside each figure as
\texttt{paper/figures/p3fig*.json}, and asserts the
regression/consistency gates recorded in
\texttt{paper/figures/p3figs-gates.json} (currently $28$; three were
added in wave 141 for the sensitivity block, and none was weakened).
They are named that way because they compare the plotted values with the
committed result files and with each other---they are not measurements,
and passing them is not evidence about the physics.
Continuous integration regenerates the figures on every change to
\texttt{paper/}, checks the regenerated data against the committed
JSON, and builds both language versions; the measurement harnesses
themselves remain outside that loop, for the reason given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:harnesses}.
