\section{Introduction}
% [第93便: 本文起こし第2弾 — 序論。位置づけ文は論文1/2 の Negative claims と整合]

Determinacy-Field Mechanics (DFM) is a two-dimensional relational toy
universe in which a single scalar field -- the determinacy $W$ sourced by
all matter -- supplies the local inertial frame, gravity, clock rates,
and optics \cite{paper1}. It is an educational \emph{computational
thought experiment}: it is not proposed as a description of the real
universe, and none of the phenomena below are claims about real
astrophysical objects (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}). Paper 1 established the
foundations and their weak-field benchmarks; Paper 2 confined the model
to a finite ``box universe'' and studied expansion, time, and light
\cite{paper2}.

This third paper asks a question that spans scales rather than sectors:
\emph{when a toy universe with only simple local rules produces
structure that looks emergent -- thermal equilibration among grains, a
heavy dark rotating centre grown from a uniform cloud, galactic-looking
disk signatures -- how do we tell robust emergence from a fixed-seed
accident?} Our answer is methodological before it is physical. We fix a
grading scheme (E0--E3) and a battery of six standard tests
(Sec.~\ref{sec:standard}), and we apply the same battery on three
scales: microscopic thermalization of an internal-spin temperature
(Sec.~\ref{sec:micro}), meso-scale self-formation of a compact dark
rotor (Sec.~\ref{sec:meso}), and macro-scale disk signatures treated
strictly as an application (Sec.~\ref{sec:macro}).

The guiding principle throughout is that \emph{a single beautiful run is
the weakest form of evidence}. Accordingly, the results below include
their own failures: an order parameter that had to be replaced after its
first version proved unmeasurable, a mass fraction that is honestly
sensitive to the softening length, an alignment timescale that outgrows
its measurement window as the particle count rises, and a heat ledger
that is only conserved once a work channel is identified and switched
off. We consider these records part of the result: they are what
distinguishes a laboratory from a gallery.

\section{A standard for emergence}
\label{sec:standard}
% [第93便: 本文起こし第1弾 — 方法論章。数値・定義はアプリ docs/PHYSICS.md と
%  ハーネス exp-4-84..93 の実測に一致させてある。査読向け英文推敲は次版]

The word ``emergence'' carries a burden of proof that a single striking
simulation cannot discharge. A fixed random seed, a particular particle
count, and a hand-tuned time window can make almost any dynamical system
produce one beautiful movie. The methodological core of this paper is
therefore a fixed standard, applied identically on every scale we study:
each claimed phenomenon must declare its order parameters in advance,
pass a battery of six standard tests, and carry an explicit grade.

\subsection{Grading}

We grade each phenomenon on a four-level scale, declared per sample and
never auto-assigned:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{E0} -- a configured display: the pattern is placed in the
  initial conditions or enforced by an external element (pinned bodies,
  rails, baths).
\item \textbf{E1} -- a driven effect: the pattern appears while an
  external element does work on the system.
\item \textbf{E2} -- emergence in a closed system: the pattern forms from
  the declared local rules alone, with momentum and angular momentum
  closing on the ledger, but robustness has not been established.
\item \textbf{E3} -- robust emergence: E2, plus reproduction across seeds
  and particle counts and recovery from perturbations, established by the
  standard tests below.
\end{itemize}

The grade is metadata attached to the sample and shown in its user
interface; the measurement harnesses that justify it are cited next to
the grade. Nothing in the engine reads the grade -- it is a claim, not a
mechanism.

\subsection{The six standard tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Knockout control.} Remove one candidate mechanism (a fusion
  rule, a conduction coefficient, a core seed) and re-run under identical
  conditions. If the phenomenon survives, the mechanism was not its
  cause; if every candidate can be removed without effect, the
  phenomenon was configured, not emergent.
\item \emph{Dose response.} Vary the strength of the claimed driver
  monotonically and require the declared order parameter to respond
  monotonically. Non-monotonic responses are reported as such (an
  example: the fusion-threshold sweep of Sec.~\ref{sec:meso}).
\item \emph{Multi-seed.} Re-run under a fixed set of random seeds
  (sixteen in this paper) and report the full range of the order
  parameter, not its best value. Controls run under the same seeds.
\item \emph{Particle-count scaling.} Repeat at several particle counts
  (three to four octaves here) to exclude discrete artefacts of one
  resolution. Where the count is entangled with a physical quantity --
  in our meso-scale sample $n$ is also the total mass -- the entanglement
  is stated rather than hidden.
\item \emph{Perturbation recovery.} Disturb the formed state -- with the
  application's own momentum-conserving injector where possible -- and
  measure whether the order parameters return. A pattern that never
  recovers is a relic of the initial conditions, not an attractor.
\item \emph{Time window.} State the window in which the claim holds and
  what happens outside it. Timescales that grow with system size or
  coupling (both occur below) are reported as part of the result.
\end{enumerate}

Two reporting rules bind all six tests. First, order parameters are
declared before testing; switching the judged quantity after a failed
run is recorded in the harness header when it happens (it happened twice
in this study -- Secs.~\ref{sec:micro} and \ref{sec:meso} -- and both
switches are documented with the failed first attempt). Second, negative
and partial results are published in the same format as positive ones:
each sample's user interface carries a ``what did not hold'' list, and
this paper reproduces those lists verbatim in
Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}.

Every test in this section runs headless from a version-controlled
harness (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}); the numbers quoted in
Secs.~\ref{sec:micro}--\ref{sec:robust} are the outputs of those
harnesses, not of interactive runs.

\subsection{System and order parameter}

The microscopic testbed is deliberately minimal: a closed box of
$120+120$ identical grains, prepared as a cold population on the left
($T_{\mathrm{int}}=0.01$) and a hot one on the right ($T_{\mathrm{int}}=6.25$),
with gravity and frame dragging off ($G=0$, $k_{\mathrm{frame}}=0$).
Temperature is the internal state variable $T_{\mathrm{int}}$ of the A9$''$
revision (heat energy $E=C\,m\,T_{\mathrm{int}}$), so ``thermalization''
here means transport of $T_{\mathrm{int}}$ by the conduction channel
E10$'$ and by collisions -- not equipartition of translational motion.
The declared order parameter is the inter-group contrast
$R(t)=\Delta T(t)/\Delta T(0)$ between the two populations; the judged
quantity is its decay rate $\lambda$, the least-squares slope of
$\ln R(t)$.

That choice of judged quantity is itself a documented switch. The first
version of the harness judged the equilibration time $t_{\mathrm{eq}}$
inside a 3000-step window and \emph{failed in every condition}: the
conduction timescale of this system is long -- extrapolating the
measured decay to $R=0.1$ gives ${\sim}6.8\times10^{4}$ steps. Rather
than widening the window until the test passed, we replaced the judged
quantity by the rate $\lambda$, recorded the failed first attempt in the
harness header (\texttt{tests/exp-4-90.mjs}), and kept the timescale
itself as the time-window result of the battery (test~6).

\subsection{The six tests}

\emph{Knockout (test 1).} With all thermal channels off
($\kappa_s=\mu_F=\gamma_n=0$) the contrast is frozen exactly:
$R=1.0000$, $\lambda=0$ over three seeds. With \emph{only} conduction
removed ($\kappa_s=0$, collisions on) the rate collapses to the noise
scale ($|\lambda| \lesssim 2\times10^{-6}$ per step, sign not even
stable): inter-group transport is carried essentially entirely by the
conduction channel, while collisions merely dissipate within each
group. The phenomenon has exactly one load-bearing mechanism, and
removing it stops the clock.

\emph{Dose response (test 2).} Sweeping $\kappa_s \in \{0.05, 0.15,
0.45\}$ moves $\lambda$ monotonically and nearly proportionally; the
global fit below gives an exponent $1.006$.

\emph{Multi-seed (test 3).} Sixteen seeds give $\lambda = 2.45\times
10^{-5}$--$4.20\times10^{-5}$ per step (spread ratio 1.71, median
$3.28\times10^{-5}$); no seed fails to relax and no seed relaxes
without the channel.

\emph{Particle-count scaling (test 4).} At $n=60/120/240$ per side the
rate increases monotonically. Jointly with the dose sweep the rates
collapse onto
\begin{equation}
\lambda \;\approx\; \lambda_0
\left(\frac{\kappa_s}{0.15}\right)^{1.006}
\left(\frac{n}{120}\right)^{1.084},
\qquad \lambda_0 = 3.28\times10^{-5}\ \text{per step},
\label{eq:microfit}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ proportional to the conduction coefficient and to the grain
density within a few percent (\texttt{tests/exp-4-91.mjs}).

\emph{Perturbation recovery (test 5).} After equilibration has begun we
disturb the state twice: (a) re-splitting the temperatures re-starts a
relaxation with rate ratios $\lambda_2/\lambda_1 = 0.96/1.15/1.20$
across seeds, and (b) a velocity kick through the application's own
momentum-conserving injector gives $\lambda_3/\lambda_1 =
1.10/1.32/1.32$. All ratios sit inside the declared acceptance band
$[0.5, 2]$: the relaxation is not a one-shot property of the prepared
initial state but a rate the mechanism re-establishes after arbitrary
interruptions.

\emph{Time window (test 6).} The honest number is the one that broke
the first harness: full equilibration takes ${\sim}7\times10^{4}$ steps
at the default coupling. We report the rate, and we report that the
timescale is long.

\subsection{A ledger-level prediction}

The battery above establishes robustness; it does not yet show the
sector is \emph{quantitative} inside the model. For that we use the
conduction-only configuration ($\mu_F=\gamma_n=k_{\mathrm{rep}}=0$),
in which the heat $Q=\sum C\,m\,T_{\mathrm{int}}$ should be exactly
conserved and the equilibrium temperature should be the
$C\,m$-weighted mean
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{eq}} \;=\; \frac{\sum_i C_i m_i T_{0,i}}{\sum_i C_i m_i}.
\label{eq:teq}
\end{equation}
Measured (\texttt{tests/exp-4-93.mjs}, two seeds each): with equal
masses the prediction is $T_{\mathrm{eq}}=3.13$ and both populations
approach it monotonically from either side while $Q$ drifts by
${\sim}10^{-6}$ (relative). The discriminating case is unequal masses
(left grains at $m=2$): the naive arithmetic mean would remain $3.13$,
but the ledger predicts $T_{\mathrm{eq}}=2.09$ -- and the measured
populations bracket and approach $2.09$, again with $Q$ conserved to
${\sim}10^{-6}$. The prediction is non-trivial (it distinguishes
mass-weighted from naive averaging) and it is derived from the same
bookkeeping that the conservation monitor displays in the application.
In the default, dissipative configuration the same ledger acquires a
collision-dissipation term; the clean statement is deliberately made in
the configuration where the channel under test is the only one open.

With the six tests and the ledger prediction, the microscopic sector
carries the full battery of Sec.~\ref{sec:standard}. What it
establishes is modest and precise: in this toy universe, inter-group
thermalization is a genuine, robust consequence of one declared channel,
with a rate law [Eq.~(\ref{eq:microfit})] and an equilibrium point
[Eq.~(\ref{eq:teq})] that survive seeds, densities, and perturbations.

\subsection{System}

The meso-scale system is a uniform disk of 180 identical grains (mass
1, spin 0, all free), given a slow global rotation
($\omega_0$) and one identical microscopic core seed inside every grain
(5\% of its mass, $J_{\mathrm{core}}=0.0010$ each). Nothing else is
placed: no central body, no dark core, no halo, no pinned particles, no
boundary, no external field. The active rules are self-gravity (E4),
contact (E9), fusion (two grains merging when their centres come within
$d<0.7\,(R_i+R_j)$), and the conservation-law inheritance of the cores
($M_c' = M_{c,i}+M_{c,j}$, $R_c' = \sqrt{R_{c,i}^2+R_{c,j}^2}$,
$J_c' = J_{c,i}+J_{c,j}$). The centre of mass and its velocity are
zeroed once at build time; thereafter the system is closed (E6$'$-R),
and the conservation monitor closes momentum and angular momentum with
all reservoir ledgers at zero ($|\Delta L|/L_{\mathrm{scale}} =
3.5\times10^{-6}$ over the reference run, $\leq 1.4\times10^{-5}$ over
all sixteen seeds).

All numbers below are quoted in the application's uniform light-speed
convention ($c_0=30$), in which this sample was re-measured in full;
the reference run is seed 20260806 evaluated at $t=288$ (18000 steps).

\subsection{Formation, and what causes it}

In the reference run the 180 grains undergo 146 fusions down to 34
bodies, and the heaviest body grows into a centre holding 27.2\% of the
total mass ($m=49$), with bound fraction 0.94. The knockout control
(fusion off) leaves the largest body at one original grain -- a mass
fraction of exactly $0.56\% = 1/180$, for every seed -- with bound
fraction 0.15: the heavy centre is a product of the fusion rule, not of
the initial conditions. The causal role of self-gravity is isolated by
a dose response: varying only $G$ over $0.5/1/1.5/2/3$ moves the mass
fraction monotonically through $1.1/7.8/18.9/27.2/57.8\%$
($7/62/125/155/173$ fusions).

The core angular momentum accumulates through the inheritance path and
nothing else: the centre's $J_{\mathrm{core}}=0.0490$ is exactly 49
seeds' worth, matching grain for grain the mass 49 it swallowed (the
sum in the inheritance rule literally counts them); the system-wide
$\sum J_{\mathrm{core}}$ stays at $0.1800 = 180\times0.0010$ to the
end; and the no-core control gives $J_{\mathrm{core}}\equiv 0$.

The darkness grows too. The effective dimming (A11/E8R -- how much of
its own light a body sweeps out by spinning) starts at $0.000$
everywhere; by $t=288$ the central body has dimmed to $0.818$ while its
surroundings average $0.192$ -- a dark centre with a bright surround,
contrast 4.3. Dimming is set purely by the depth of the well
$\psi=W/K_t$ and never enters the dynamics: sweeping $K_t$ over
$12/14/16/20/24$ leaves particle count, fusion count, mass fraction,
spin and radius identical to the last bit while the dimming alone moves
through $1.000/0.818/0.574/0.349/0.251$. The grains start with zero
spin and end co-rotating unanimously (spin alignment
$0.000\rightarrow1.000$, clump count $166\rightarrow2$, largest-clump
mass fraction 0.994, rotation support $V/\sigma=11.2$).

\subsection{The six tests}

\emph{Multi-seed (test 3).} Over sixteen seeds the central mass
fraction spans 16.7--47.8\% (median 26.7\%) and is at least 16.7\% in
every seed, while the no-fusion control sits at 0.56\% for all sixteen:
the worst seed is 30 times its control. Across the same seeds:
largest-clump mass fraction 0.978--1.000, spin alignment 1.000 in every
seed, $V/\sigma$ 4.1--22.0, central dimming 0.513--1.000 against
surround 0.156--0.220, $J_{\mathrm{core}}$ 0.0300--0.0860 (30--86
seeds' worth), bound fraction 0.49--0.94, 26--42 bodies left
(138--154 fusions), no NaN, no clamping. The discriminating optical
quantity is the \emph{contrast} (centre over surround): 2.59--5.41
(median 4.11) in the main runs against 0.36--1.26 (median 1.16) in the
controls -- the two ranges never overlap.

\emph{Knockout and dose (tests 1--2)} are the fusion knockout, the
no-core control, and the $G$ dose above; a second dose response in the
fusion threshold $d_{\mathrm{frac}}$ ($0.7/0.6/0.5/0.35 \rightarrow
27.2/37.2/53.9/53.9\%$) is monotone-then-saturating \emph{in this
realization} -- in the previous parameterization the same sweep
measured non-monotonic ($26.7/47.8/43.3/23.3\%$). The very shape of
this dose curve is sensitive to the rounding realization, and we record
that rather than choosing the prettier curve.

\emph{Particle-count scaling (test 4).} At $n=90/180/360$ (three
representative seeds) the median mass fraction is 17.8/22.8/37.5\%,
largest-clump fraction 0.933/0.994/1.000, alignment 1.000 throughout.
An honest entanglement is stated rather than hidden: this sample keeps
the disk radius and per-grain mass fixed, so $n$ is also the total mass
-- $n=90$ acts like halving $G$, and the ten-times-control bar is met
there by only 2 of the 3 seeds (ratios 2.0/16.0/20.0).

\emph{Perturbation recovery (test 5).} Gaussian velocity noise injected
at $t=240$ through the application's momentum-conserving injector, at
30\% of the speed RMS, moves none of the order parameters; sweeping the
amplitude, the largest clump only starts to break at four times the
speed RMS ($0.994\rightarrow0.861$; at twice it stays 0.994). What is
disturbed and returns is $V/\sigma$ (down 39--61\%, back within
20--100 steps) and the bound fraction (down $\leq$15\%, back within
20--50 steps; one seed misses the recovery criterion inside the
measured window -- recorded). Velocity noise cannot touch the
alignment by construction, so a separate perturbation flips the spins
of about half the grains: alignment is driven from 1.000 to
$0.531/-0.212/0.084$ and recovers within 20 steps ($\Delta t=0.32$).
The co-rotating state is not a configuration but an attractor that
contact friction (E9) and dragging (A8/E6$'$) re-establish every step.

\emph{Time window (test 6).} The clumps merge into one by step 3000
($t=48$); alignment passes 0.99 by steps 4000--5000; the mass fraction
reaches 90\% of its final value between steps 4000 and 6000 and then
saturates -- at step 18000 (the declared window) and step 36000 it
differs by at most 0.6 points. The window sits inside the plateau, past
the rise.

\subsection{Numerical robustness, with a generation note}

Three further sweeps probe the integration rather than the physics.
They were recorded (\texttt{tests/exp-4-89.mjs},
\texttt{exp-4-92.mjs}; 2026-08-08) in the sample's \emph{previous}
parameterization, before its exact-similarity rescaling into the
$c_0=30$ convention (velocities and rates $\times k$, times $\div k$;
the continuum dynamics is identical by construction, the discrete
integration differs slightly, and the multi-seed distribution above was
re-measured after the rescaling). We quote them in their original
units, against the old-unit sixteen-seed floor of 18.3\%:

(i) \emph{Timestep}: $dt \in \{0.016, 0.008, 0.004\}$ at fixed physical
time leaves every order parameter inside the sixteen-seed spread with
$|\Delta L|/L_{\mathrm{scale}}\sim10^{-6}$; the fusion count drifts
mildly ($155\rightarrow147\rightarrow138$). (ii) \emph{Softening}:
$\times0.5/\times1/\times2$ -- formation survives everywhere
(largest-clump 0.978--1.000, alignment 1.000), but the mass fraction
\emph{is} softening-sensitive at $\times2$ (fusions drop to 104--122;
minimum mass fraction 11.1\%, below the sixteen-seed floor):
quantitative mass fractions must be quoted at fixed softening, and we
do so. (iii) \emph{$n=720$}: formation completes (largest-clump 1.000,
control ratio 166--208$\times$) and the mass fraction saturates
(median 31.5\% vs.\ 31.7\% at $n=360$), but the alignment timescale
grows with $n$ -- one seed reaches only 0.752 by step 9000 and 1.000 by
step 13500. The $t$-window was tuned at $n=180$; at $n=720$ it is a
caveat, and we record it as such.

\subsection{What the meso chapter claims}

A heavy, dark, co-rotating, angular-momentum-bearing centre forms from
a uniform cloud of identical grains in a closed system, with every
ingredient of the outcome traced to a declared rule by knockout or dose
response, reproduced across sixteen seeds and three particle counts,
recovering from perturbations, and stated with its two honest
sensitivities (softening; alignment timescale at large $n$). By the
grading of Sec.~\ref{sec:standard} this is E3: robust emergence --
\emph{of the toy universe's own dark rotor}, and of nothing in the real
sky (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}).
