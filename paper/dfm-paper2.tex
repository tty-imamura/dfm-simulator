\title{The Box Universe: Expansion, Time, and Light in a Relational Toy Model}

\author{Tetsuya Imamura}
\affiliation{Independent researcher, Japan}

\date{2026-07-31 (draft v0.8)}

\begin{abstract}
We extend Determinacy-Field Mechanics (DFM), a two-dimensional
relational toy model introduced in a companion paper, by introducing a
\emph{box universe} approximation: everything outside a finite region
is replaced by a single simplified background.
The box makes classic relational questions computable---uniform
translation of the box leaves the specified interior observables
bit-identical, while rotation and expansion couple through a measured
radial gain that approaches the analytic center limit $g(0)=1/2$---and
separates expansion into three machine-verified layers: prescribed
kinematics, wavelength semantics, and solved dynamics.
The central result is an \emph{estimator bias under a mismatched decay
law}: a geometric probe and a velocity-decay probe applied to the same
box return expansion rates whose ratio is the analytic factor
$H_w/H_{\mathrm{geo}}=2(D/K_t)\,d_P\,a^{-d_P}$, because the velocity
observer fits the FLRW rule $w/c\propto1/a$ to data generated by an
exponential law.
The measured ratio matches the analytic factor to $5.3\times10^{-4}$,
and the discrepancy pattern furnishes a structural analogy to
probe-dependent determinations of the Hubble constant, discussed---and
deliberately bounded---in the Discussion.
All central simulation-derived quantitative claims are tied to
automated verification gates that run in continuous integration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The companion paper~\cite{DFM1} introduced Determinacy-Field
Mechanics (DFM), an educational toy model in which space is purely relational: every
mechanical property of a point is set by the surrounding masses
through a scalar \emph{determinacy field} $W$, and inertia, gravity,
clock rate, and light propagation are all read from it.
That paper studied local consequences---frame dragging, rotation
curves, clocks, and lensing---on an effectively infinite background.

This paper studies the opposite end of the approximation: what happens
when ``the rest of the universe'' is deliberately replaced by a single
simplified object, which we call the \emph{box}.
The box universe is not a new axiom; it is the statement that beyond a
chosen radius the determinacy sources are summarized by a uniform
background term $D_0$, a box determinacy $D$ with scale factor $a(t)$,
and optionally a uniform gravity vector.
Every cosmological question the toy model can ask---What expands?
What is comoving? What does a redshift mean? Can expansion be
\emph{solved} rather than prescribed?---becomes a question about the
box, and, because the model is small and deterministic, each answer
can be machine-verified.

Our position is unchanged from the companion paper: DFM is an
educational \emph{computational thought experiment}, not a proposal
about nature (Sec.~\ref{sec:negative}).
The interest of the box universe is that it makes textbook
distinctions---kinematic expansion versus dynamic expansion,
coordinate recession versus measured redshift, probe versus
quantity---concrete enough for a student to toggle in a browser, with
a continuous-integration suite asserting the central numbers in this
paper.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} defines the model, the numerical methods,
and the error taxonomy used throughout.
Section~\ref{sec:box} defines the box and establishes what the inside
can and cannot detect.
Section~\ref{sec:layers} separates expansion into three verified
layers.
Section~\ref{sec:probe} contains the main result: probe-dependent
expansion estimates.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the estimator-bias reading and
its deliberately bounded analogy with the Hubble tension.
Section~\ref{sec:negative} states six negative claims that are part of
the thesis, and Sec.~\ref{sec:repro} maps every central claim to its
verification gate and states code and data availability.
Appendix~\ref{app:dark} applies the same machinery to the model's
optically dark gravitating bodies.

\section{Model and numerical methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Fields, forces, clocks, and light}

DFM is a two-dimensional particle model.
Point masses $m_j$ at positions $\bm r_j$ source a scalar
\emph{determinacy field}
\begin{equation}
  W(\bm r)=D_0+W_B+\sum_j \frac{m_j}{\sqrt{|\bm r-\bm r_j|^2+
  \epsilon^2}} ,
  \label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is a Plummer softening length, $D_0$ a uniform
background, and $W_B$ the box term defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:box}.
Gravity is Newtonian pairwise attraction with the same softening;
a spinning mass drags the local \emph{determinacy frame} with a
velocity field whose strength is set by a global coupling
$k_{\mathrm{Frame}}\in[0,1]$, and a moving background transports free
particles through the same channel (the model's Machian sector).
Clock rate and light propagation are both read from the single
dimensionless potential $\psi=W/K_t$: proper time advances as
$d\tau/dt=e^{-\psi}$ and the local light speed is
$c_{\mathrm{loc}}=c_0\,e^{-2\psi}$, so the effective refractive index
is $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=e^{2\psi}$.
$W$, $D$, $D_0$ carry the (arbitrary) simulation mass unit divided by
length; $K_t$ carries the same unit, making $\psi$ dimensionless; all
lengths, times, and speeds are in internal simulation units throughout
(none of the claims below depend on a physical calibration of those
units).
Optional sectors used in this paper: a pressure-like pair repulsion
between spinning or heated particles with far-field force
$F\propto d^{\,1-2q}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:layers}), dissipative contact
(normal damping and translation-to-spin friction), conduction, and
radiative cooling; each is switched off wherever it is not explicitly
listed as active.

\subsection{Integration and state}

The equations of motion are integrated with a first-order
semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler scheme at a fixed step
$\Delta t=0.016$; a time-symmetric leapfrog (KDK) variant is available
and is used only where reversibility itself is under test.
All state is double precision.
Safety clamps (speed, spin, and expansion-rate bounds) exist as
divergence guards; a ledger counts their activations, and a
verification gate asserts \emph{zero activations} in every
configuration for which conservation is claimed.
Momentum, angular momentum, and energy books are closed against
explicit reservoir ledgers; conservation-type gates assert the closed
sums, not merely small drifts.

\subsection{Measured quantities}

The observables used in this paper are defined once and reused
everywhere.
The \emph{geometric expansion rate} over a window
$[t_1,t_2]$ is $H_{\mathrm{geo}}=\Delta\ln a/\Delta t$.
The \emph{velocity-decay estimate} is
$H_w=-\Delta\ln R/\Delta t$ with $R=w/c_{\mathrm{loc}}$, where $w$ is
the particle's peculiar speed relative to the prescribed Hubble flow.
Window averages are always quoted with their windows; a
\emph{cumulative} estimator (referenced to $t=0$) and a
\emph{local-window} estimator are distinguished wherever both appear.
The free box's \emph{effective scale factor} is
$a_{\mathrm{eff}}=\langle r_{\mathrm{wall}}\rangle/\langle
r_{\mathrm{wall}}\rangle_0$, measured from the wall masses.
Observer-plane flux and capture rates for ray experiments are defined
in Appendix~\ref{app:dark}.

\subsection{Error taxonomy and verification independence}
\label{sec:taxonomy}

Each machine-checked claim carries one of five verification types,
declared in the code next to the claim itself:
\emph{analytic match} (agreement with a closed-form expression),
\emph{conservation identity} (ledger-closed sums),
\emph{semantic regression} (a definition or display rule),
\emph{fixed-seed run} (a deterministic single-run measurement), and
\emph{multi-seed statistics} (spread across seeds; quoted $\pm$ values
are between-realization standard deviations).
Two honesty notes accompany this machinery.
First, most figure data are generated by driving the simulator itself,
which guarantees figure--implementation synchrony but is not an
independent proof of the implementation; the analytic-match gates
compare against short closed-form expressions (evaluated in the test
harness, outside the engine), and the conservation identities are
independent of the analytic predictions.
Second, several gate tolerances were set \emph{after} the first
measurement; those gates are regression baselines that pin the
recorded value, not blind predictions, and are labeled fixed-seed
accordingly.

Discretization was probed directly for the central result: halving and
re-halving the time step changes the probe-ratio error of
Sec.~\ref{sec:probe} monotonically from $5.3\times10^{-4}$
($\Delta t=0.016$) to $2.7\times10^{-4}$ ($0.008$) and
$1.1\times10^{-4}$ ($0.004$), consistent with the first-order
integrator; the quoted tolerance of $10^{-2}$ sits two orders above
the measured error at the production step.

\section{The box as an approximation}
\label{sec:box}

\subsection{Semantics}
\label{sec:semantics}

The box universe is defined by six semantic commitments (canonical
statement in the project's box-universe document, Sec.~12):
(1)~outside a finite region, determinacy sources are summarized, not
simulated;
(2)~the summary consists of a uniform background $D_0$, a box term
$W_B=D\,a^{-d_P}$ carried by a scale factor $a(t)$, and optionally a
uniform gravity vector---conceptually these are all \emph{box
parameters};
(3)~interior physics is unchanged: the same axioms read the total
field $W$ wherever it comes from;
(4)~the box may be \emph{prescribed} (a regulated $a(t)$) or
\emph{free} (walls made of ordinary masses whose motion solves
$a(t)$);
(5)~comoving coordinates and wavelength semantics are definitions
layered on top, each with its own verification; and
(6)~no statement about the real universe is implied by any of it.

\subsection{What the inside cannot detect}
\label{sec:t10}

Because every axiom of the model depends only on relative
configuration (Eq.~\ref{eq:W} and the pairwise force laws are
functions of coordinate differences), uniform translation of the
entire box is a symmetry of the dynamics; the verification suite
additionally asserts, at machine precision, that the implemented
interior evolution is bit-identical under a boosted box for the
tracked observables (positions, velocities, spins, clocks).
We therefore claim exact undetectability for the specified observables
of the implementation, with the analytic symmetry argument carrying
the generalization.
Rotation and expansion are different: they enter through the
determinacy frame with a measured \emph{gain} $g(r)$.
For a uniform ring of wall masses the analytic average of the
direction-projected position over sources gives
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\langle \hat n\,(\hat n\cdot\bm r)\bigr\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\bm r ,
  \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
so the interior center couples to wall rotation and expansion with
gain $g(0)=1/2$.
The value at the center is an analytic limit (the measured estimator
is $0/0$ there); the innermost measured point of the radial scan,
$r=20$, gives $g=0.5002$, and the agreement of the full scan with the
analytic curve supports the limit (gates V23a/V24a).
This is the toy-model version of the classic relational
statement~\cite{Mach1893,BarbourBertotti1982}---an analogy, not the
same theoretical conclusion: absolute uniform
motion is meaningless, but absolute rotation and expansion of
``everything else'' are interior-observable through the frame they
drag.
Figure~\ref{fig:hierarchy} sketches the construction and
Fig.~\ref{fig:gain} shows the measured gain against the analytic
uniform-ring curve.

\section{Three layers of expansion}
\label{sec:layers}

\subsection{Layer 1: prescribed kinematics}

With a regulated $a(t)$ the box provides FLRW-style bookkeeping
without dynamics.
The comoving coordinate $\chi=r/a$ of a fully comoving particle is
conserved to $1.5\times10^{-5}$ over the verification run (V24b).
Bound systems follow the box only fractionally: the measured orbit
scaling $d\propto a^{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ has an exponent that grows
with the determinacy
share $\varphi_B$ of the box at the orbit (V25: net exponent $1.61$
at $\varphi_B{\to}1$ and $0.00$ at $\varphi_B{\to}0$), giving the
textbook ``bound systems do not expand'' as the $\varphi_B\to 0$ end
of a measured dial (Fig.~\ref{fig:phib}).

  expansion susceptibility $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of a bound binary
  ($d\propto a^{n}$) against the box's determinacy share
  $\varphi_B=W_B/(W_B+w_{\mathrm{local}})$, measured with the V25
  construction (prescribed $H=0.004$ box, run to $a=1.2$, differenced
  against an $H=0$ control so that internal dissipation cancels). The
  scan holds the binary fixed ($m=2000$, $d=24$) and varies only the

  rough energy-pumping estimate $n=2\varphi_B$, which the measurement

\subsection{Layer 2: wavelength semantics}

Redshift in the box is a \emph{semantic} rule: emitted light retains
the wavelength ratio $1+z=a_{\mathrm{obs}}/a_{\mathrm{emit}}$.
The suite emits light from two comoving sources A and B at different
epochs of the same run and checks both arrivals at a third comoving
observer C nearly simultaneously ($\Delta t=0.080$; gate \texttt{box.photon-abc}, bookkeeping
closed below $10^{-9}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:abc}).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{p2fig4.pdf}
  \caption{The A/B/C thought experiment, run
  (preset \texttt{boxredshift}, $H=0.02$, $d_P=1$).
  (a)~Wavelengths in flight, $\lambda(t)=\lambda_E\,a(t)/a_E$. A emits
  $500$\,nm at $t=0$ ($a=1$) and B emits $500$\,nm at $t=34.657$
  ($a=2$); both are observed at C at $t\approx68.2$ with
  $1959$\,nm ($z=2.919$) and $978$\,nm ($z=0.956$).
  (b)~World lines. After B's emission the two photons follow the same
  path, so light emitted at different epochs arrives together carrying
  different redshifts. The measured ledger
  $|\lambda_{\mathrm{obs}}/\lambda_{\mathrm{emit}}-
  a_{\mathrm{obs}}/a_{\mathrm{emit}}|$ closes to machine zero, which
  is the content of negative claim~2: this is a rule, not a
  derivation.}
  \label{fig:abc}
\end{figure}
This is a definition layered on the kinematics, not a derivation from
photon dynamics (negative claim 2 of this paper); its value is that
it \emph{encodes} the same scale-factor dependence that appears in
the observed consistency relations---supernova time
dilation $\propto(1+z)$~\cite{Leibundgut1996,Goldhaber2001} and
$T_{\mathrm{CMB}}(z)=T_0(1+z)$~\cite{Fixsen2009,Noterdaeme2011}---while
those observables themselves are not simulated here; a student can
see exactly which part of such relations is semantics and which is
physics.

\subsection{Layer 3: solved dynamics --- the free box}

Replacing the regulated box by 64 free wall masses turns $a(t)$ into
an output.
The driver is a \emph{pressure-like pair repulsion} between spinning
wall masses---not a thermodynamic equation of state or a
stress-energy pressure; the term ``pressure'' is used in this
loose, mechanism-level sense throughout.
With the repulsion on, the measured effective scale factor
accelerates to $a_{\mathrm{eff}}=2.18$; with it
removed ($k_{\mathrm{Rep}}=0$) the same box coasts to
$a_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx1.005$ and recollapses.
The far-field pair repulsion scales as $F\propto d^{\,1-2q}$; this
preset uses $q=1$, for which it decays as $1/d$---slower than
gravity, so it wins at large separation (the DFM default $q=2$ gives
$1/d^{3}$ and would not).
The specific value $2.18$ is a property of this discrete ring
configuration
($q=1$, $k_{\mathrm{Rep}}=0.5$, 64 equal wall masses), not a
universal number; the in-app A/B controls expose its dose response,
and a four-quadrant $G\times k_{\mathrm{Rep}}$ scan pins the source
of the acceleration on the repulsion itself (gravity's braking
contribution to $a_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is $-0.013$).
The run is a fully closed system: reservoir bookkeeping is
identically zero and momentum/angular-momentum drift is below
$4\times10^{-8}$ (gates \texttt{freebox.*}).
This is a mechanism demonstration in the spirit of Friedmann
cosmology---attraction decelerates, pressure-like repulsion can
accelerate---with no claim of reproducing the Friedmann equations
(negative claim 5); Fig.~\ref{fig:freebox}.

\subsection{Aside: an arrow of time in a reversible box}

With the leapfrog integrator the dynamics are operationally
reversible to RMS $1.3\times10^{-3}$ over this finite test: flipping
all velocities and spins returns the initial state to that accuracy.
Turning on one dissipative channel ($\mu_F=0.3$) destroys the return
(RMS $10.8$); the default semi-implicit integrator, though
deterministic, also fails to rewind (RMS $0.92$, a $709\times$
degradation).
The experiment thus separates determinism from reversibility:
dissipation is one source of an arrow, and a non-time-symmetric
discretization creates an additional, purely numerical arrow (gates
\texttt{echo.*}).
This section is deliberately brief; a fuller treatment is future
work.

\section{Probe-dependent expansion estimates}
\label{sec:probe}

\subsection{Hubble anti-friction and the ratio standard}

In DFM the peculiar velocity $w$ of a free particle is conserved in
the absolute bookkeeping of the model even while the box expands
(measured drift $<10^{-3}$; gates V27/V28).
What decays is the \emph{ratio} $R=w/c_{\mathrm{loc}}$: the local
light speed rises as the box determinacy dilutes,
$c_{\mathrm{loc}}=c_0\,e^{-2\psi}$ with $\psi=W_B/K_t$, giving
\begin{equation}
  R(a)=\exp\!\Bigl[-\tfrac{2D}{K_t}\bigl(1-a^{-d_P}\bigr)\Bigr],
  \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
verified to $2.5\times10^{-4}$ at $a=2$ (V28).
Measured against light---one operational standard available to an
interior observer---free motion \emph{does} decay, in the same direction as the
FLRW $w\propto1/a$ friction~\cite{Peacock1999}, although with a
different functional form.

\subsection{Two probes, one box}
\label{sec:v29}

Now let two observers measure the expansion rate of the same box.
The \emph{geometric} observer tracks comoving structure and reports
$H_{\mathrm{geo}}=\Delta\ln a/\Delta t$.
The \emph{velocity} observer assumes FLRW kinematics
($w/c\propto1/a$) and infers an expansion rate from the measured
decay of $R$:
$H_w=-\Delta\ln R/\Delta t$.
From Eq.~(\ref{eq:R}),
\begin{equation}
  \frac{H_w}{H_{\mathrm{geo}}}=\frac{2D}{K_t}\,d_P\,a^{-d_P},
  \label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
which depends on the epoch $a$ and on the box calibration $D/K_t$.
The two probes disagree, not because either is wrong, but because the
model's decay law is not the FLRW one the second observer assumed.

Verification gate V29 measures both probes in one run over three
windows ($a\colon1\to1.5\to2\to3$, $d_P=1$) and compares the measured
ratio with the log-window average of Eq.~(\ref{eq:ratio}); the
largest relative error is $5.3\times10^{-4}$ against a tolerance of
$10^{-2}$, with $w$ conserved to $4.8\times10^{-4}$.
The broader 24-window scan behind Fig.~\ref{fig:probeH} reaches a
maximum relative deviation of $6.2\times10^{-3}$; the two accuracies
are distinct, and both satisfy the predeclared $10^{-2}$ tolerance.
The error is discretization-dominated and converges with the step
(Sec.~\ref{sec:taxonomy}).
Figure~\ref{fig:probeH} extends the gate into an
$a\times(D/K_t)$ scan, following the comparison design 4-66 of the
project's experiment ledger; that comparison experiment is
implemented as a standalone script whose recorded run meets all four
predeclared criteria (exponential-series accuracy, control identity,
curve separation above $10\sigma$, and the V29 regression gate), and
its output doubles as the data source for Fig.~6.

The functional form is the entire content of the effect.
If the model's light-speed law were replaced by the exact inverse
proportionality $c_{\mathrm{loc}}\propto1/W$, then
$R\propto a^{-d_P}$ exactly, both probes would coincide identically
($H_w/H_{\mathrm{geo}}\equiv d_P$), and the probe dependence would
vanish.
The choice between the two laws is thus \emph{observable from inside
the model}, which is the pedagogical point: what looks like a
measurement discrepancy can be a statement about which decay law
nature (here: the axiom set) actually uses.

One point must be stated carefully (it was mis-stated in an earlier
draft and caught in external review): for $d_P=1$ the ratio
$\rho(a)=2(D/K_t)/a$ decreases monotonically, but the probe
\emph{mismatch} $|\rho-1|$ does not.
The probes agree exactly once, at $a=2D/K_t\approx1.85$; the mismatch
shrinks up to that epoch and grows again thereafter, with
$H_w/H_{\mathrm{geo}}\to0$ as $a\to\infty$.
The model therefore provides probe dependence, not a monotonic
early-to-late relaxation of the discrepancy.

\section{Discussion: estimator bias, and a bounded analogy}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result of Sec.~\ref{sec:probe} is best read as a worked example
of \emph{estimator misspecification}: two internally consistent
estimators of the same expansion applied to the same data disagree
because one of them fits the wrong decay law.
Nothing in the construction depends on the toy model's specifics---the
lesson transfers to any setting where an inferred rate inherits the
functional form its estimator assumes.

The analogy with the real Hubble tension is structural and
deliberately bounded.
Two representative determinations of the Hubble constant
disagree: $H_0=67.4\pm0.5\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ from the
CMB fit under base $\Lambda$CDM~\cite{Planck2018} versus
$H_0=73.04\pm1.04\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ from the 2022 local
distance ladder~\cite{Riess2022}, a ratio of $0.923$; the 2024
JWST-crossvalidated ladder value $73.2\pm0.9$~\cite{Riess2024} gives
$0.921$, and the wider landscape of methods spans a range of such
ratios~\cite{DiValentino2021}---the comparison ratio depends on which
pair of determinations one adopts.
In the box universe, choosing $D/K_t=\tfrac{2}{3}\ln 4\approx 0.9242$
(the value that makes $R$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:R}) fall to exactly $1/4$
at $a=4$) makes the probe ratio at $a=2$ equal $D/K_t\approx0.92$
identically---a fractional difference of $7.6\%$ between the two
estimates, the same magnitude as the observed ratios above.
This is a calibration, chosen when the 2022 pair was adopted as the
reference, and we do not retune it as observational values evolve;
the numeric agreement reduces to the identity
$2^{4/3}\approx e^{0.9233}$, true to $0.10\%$---a coincidence of
constants, which is why negative claim~6 treats it as calibration,
not evidence.
One further honesty note: $\psi=D/K_t\approx0.92$
is a \emph{strong-field} setting of the toy
($c_{\mathrm{loc}}/c_0=e^{-2\psi}\approx0.16$), far outside the
weak-field regime $|\psi|\ll1$ in which the companion paper's GR
calibrations live; nothing in this section inherits those
calibrations.
What the model \emph{is}: a minimal, fully inspectable mechanism in
which the measured expansion rate depends on the probe---global fits
and local kinematics need not agree if the underlying decay law
differs from the assumed one.
What it \emph{is not}: an explanation of the Hubble tension---the
model is two-dimensional, non-Lorentzian, and the coincidence of
$0.92$ with $0.92$--$0.923$ is a calibration, not a prediction
(negative claims 3 and 6).

\section{Limitations and negative claims}
\label{sec:negative}

As in the companion paper, these are part of the thesis.

\subsection{Negative claim 1}
The box universe is not a description of the real universe: the model
is two-dimensional, has no Lorentz invariance, and no causal
propagation of the fields.

\subsection{Negative claim 2}
The wavelength-retention rule is semantics, not a derivation from
photon dynamics.

\subsection{Negative claim 3}
Probe-dependent $H$ is presented as a structural analogy; no claim is
made that it explains the Hubble tension.

\subsection{Negative claim 4}
The optically dark bodies of Appendix~\ref{app:dark} are not proposed
as candidates for real dark matter; they meet requirements
\emph{inside the model} only.

\subsection{Negative claim 5}
The free box demonstrates a mechanism (attraction decelerates,
pressure-like repulsion accelerates); it does not reproduce the
Friedmann equations.

\subsection{Negative claim 6}
$D/K_t=0.92$ is a calibration chosen to match the tension ratio's
magnitude; the numeric agreement is not evidence for anything.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A single deliberately crude approximation---replace everything far
away by a box---is enough to make the standard cosmological
distinctions computable and auditable: what is undetectable
(translation) versus gain-coupled (rotation, expansion); what is
prescribed versus semantic versus solved about expansion; and, at the
center of this paper, the fact that \emph{expansion-rate estimates
inherit the decay law the estimator assumes}: a probe that fits the
wrong functional form reports a different $H$, with the entire effect
carried by one decay law.
Every central claim is asserted by an automated gate
(Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}), and the model runs in a browser, so each
claim can be re-derived, re-measured, or broken by any reader with
the repository and a laptop.

Future work is confined to two items: an optional light probe
$H_{\mathrm{light}}$ alongside the two probes of Sec.~\ref{sec:v29},
and a fuller treatment of the reversibility demonstration.

\section{Optically dark gravitating bodies}
\label{app:dark}

The companion paper showed that DFM's rotating cores enhance outer
rotation curves through frame dragging (measured outer boost
$1.331\pm0.009$ over eight seeds on the submission target, beta
v1.34; the $\pm$ is the between-realization standard deviation across
seeds, not a model uncertainty).
This appendix assembles the model's \emph{darkness} mechanisms and
asks, inside the model, which phenomenological requirements of dark
matter a single object can meet.
It is deliberately separated from the main text: the box-universe
results stand without it.

\subsection{Two independent optical mechanisms}

``Dark'' is used operationally throughout: a body is dark
to the extent that (i)~its \emph{self-emission} is suppressed and
(ii)~its interaction with \emph{external} rays suppresses capture
and sweeps the ray fan aside.
What is measured is capture suppression, angular sweep, and the
resulting \emph{observer-plane flux}: a
vertical screen at $x=+300$ opposite the ray source counts which rays
of the standard 50-ray fan arrive on-axis ($|y|\le60$; straight-line
geometric baseline $0.28$).
Under the physical lock $K_t=c^2/G$ the screen is bright (arrival
rate $1.00$, on-axis fraction $0.30$, slightly focused above the
baseline); the non-spinning strong-field core darkens it by capture
(on-axis $0.04$, one seventh of the baseline); and the fast-spinning
core stays dark at the observer even with capture eliminated
(on-axis $0.12$), because the sweep redistributes the fan in angle
toward the retrograde side (mean crossing ordinate $+189$, mean
direction deviation $0.84$\,rad; gate \texttt{ray.observer-flux}).
These on-axis fractions are ray-grid resolutions, not continuous
probabilities: rescanning with 50--400 rays leaves the spinning-core
value at $0.115$--$0.12$ and keeps the non-spinning value within
$0.04$--$0.09$, both far below the $0.28$ baseline, so the darkness
conclusion is resolution-robust.
Absorption, scattering, transmission, and lensing are distinct
observables; the model implements the two channels below and makes no
claim about the others.

\emph{Self-light dimming} (parameter \texttt{lightSweep}) attenuates
the light a body emits; since v1.33 it can be computed from the spin
state as $l_{S,\mathrm{eff}}=\min(1,|s|R/c_{\mathrm{surf}})$ rather
than set by hand, and for the model's black-hole-like preset the
mechanistic value is $0.56$ against the toy value $1$---a gap the
interface exposes rather than hides.
\emph{External-ray sweep-out} is a strong-field effect of a rapidly
spinning core on \emph{incoming} rays: the finite-time capture that a
non-spinning core produces (measured non-escape rate $0.66$)
disappears as the core spins up, switching off between $s=0.6$ and
$s=0.7$ ($0.70\to0.08$) and reaching $0.00$ by $s=1.5$; and the
effect vanishes entirely under the physical lock $K_t=c^2/G$
(measured; the mechanism lives in the toy's strong regime only).
The two mechanisms are machine-verified to be orthogonal: ray
endpoints agree to all digits as \texttt{lightSweep} is scanned from
0 to 1 (project derivations, Sec.~17), and the self-light channel is
already saturated at $s=0.3$, where no sweep-out has begun
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}).

\subsection{Requirements met, and limits}

Inside the model, a heavy, fast-spinning, optically dark core
(i)~sources $W$ and hence gravity; (ii)~is dark by two independent
mechanisms; and (iii)~contributes to outer rotation-curve enhancement
through dragging, with the radius-resolved profile showing the boost
concentrated inward ($v/v_{\mathrm{bar}}$ enhancement from $1.58$ at
$r\approx121$ to $1.16$ at $r\approx251$, eight-seed statistics;
Fig.~\ref{fig:curve}).
It therefore meets, \emph{inside the model}, a deliberately limited
checklist---suppressed self-emission, removal of external rays, a
gravitating mass, and a rotation-curve contribution---which is far
short of the full phenomenology required of real dark matter
(mass-to-light ratios, collisionlessness, lensing, structure
formation~\cite{BertoneHooper2018}).
The limits are measured with the same care: the inverse-design
problem (choose parameters to hit a prescribed outer velocity) is
accurate as a forward predictor (median residual ${\approx}2\%$) but
saturates in azimuthal velocity before reaching the outermost bands,
and earlier claims that dragging alone sustains a specific galaxy
preset's arms were withdrawn when knockout tests showed a composite
pathway.
None of this licenses any claim about real dark matter (negative
claim 4); the value is that requirement-level talk about darkness
becomes an explicit, falsifiable checklist in a system small enough
to audit.
